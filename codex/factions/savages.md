# The Savages

## Description

Large humanoid creatures — significantly larger and stronger than humans, with fast sprint capability. Gray-skinned (as described in the horde). They have glowing eyes (Marissa encounters a female with glowing violet eyes). They communicate in a "language not meant for human throats" — guttural, possibly functional.

They absorb magic from their environment — this is their primary sustenance, not food. They can drain active spells (fire spells wink out, shields erode) but they cannot prevent the physical after-effects of magic (fire that has already ignited material keeps burning). They do not cast spells but have developed tools (crude wooden ladders, javelins, clubs, spears). They tend egg chambers with care, using carved stone nests packed with wood shavings.

## Goals

Absorb magical energy. Protect eggs. Expand where magic is densest.

## Structure

No identified hierarchy observed. They responded collectively — when Marissa's eggs were thrown at them they all stopped to catch them. Leadership unknown but implied (they executed a sophisticated trap at the focusing tower in chapter 21).

## Resources

Numbers — tens of thousands potentially, with eggs hatching rapidly when magic field is dense. Physical strength. Magic absorption. Cave fortifications (passive).

## Methods

Ambush (focusing tower trap, chapter 21). Horde assault (chapter 24). Herding prey (chapter 22 cave pursuit). Javelin and club combat. Crude but effective ladder assault on walls.

## Notes

- Kendall in ch28 believes they are descended from pre-Apocalypse human experiments — people magically modified to need only magic for sustenance, resulting in reduced intelligence and increased aggression. The humans of that era then hunted them to near-extinction.
- Marissa's discovery: physical fire (started by magic) persists even after the spell's power is drained. This is their key vulnerability.
- They show emotional responses: grieving over destroyed eggs, hesitating when eggs were threatened.
- Marissa's epilogue mission includes understanding and possibly helping them rather than exterminating them.
