# The Skylands

## Description

### Primary material rule (canonical)

**There is exactly one construction material on the Skylands: materialised mana.** Every wall, floor, ceiling, building, vehicle, weapon, gun-frame, blade, fitting, bracket, socket, hinge, casing, sword-guard, projectile, gorget plate, corset plate, helmet, sword, dispenser unit, lever, ladder rung, table, bench, headband, headband-clip, hatch latch, and lamp housing on the entire city is materialised mana shaped, hardened, layered, or laminated to perform the function required.

**Materialised mana is always in the purple-violet family — never any other colour.** The full visible range runs from near-black indigo (deepest, hardest, the structural crystal of the city walls and the Surface monolith), through deep violet (the standard weapon-frame and Powerstone-facing grade), through lighter purple (the cheaper grades, the labour-goods, the everyday-tool grade), to a milky lavender (the most translucent variant, used for optics and glow-globe lenses). **The variation is in translucency, not in hue.** A solid indigo crystal weapon-frame and a near-opaque deep-violet armour plate and a milky-lavender lamp-lens are the same material in different processing states. There is no orange, no warm tone, no red, no green, no yellow, no blue-that-isn't-violet, no brown. The Skylands palette is purple top to bottom.

**Crystal can functionally emulate any material a Skylander needs, but it does not look like that other material.** A revolver-frame is *crystal* hardened to take chamber pressure and rifled into a barrel; it does not "look like steel" because it does not look like steel — it looks like a deep-violet hard crystal worked into the shape of a revolver. A coat is *crystal* spun into a fibre that drapes; it does not "look like wool" because Skylanders have no referent for wool. A door-hinge is *crystal* hardened, machined, and lubricated to swing on; it does not "look like brass" because Skylanders have no referent for brass.

**Kyra does not know what brass, iron, steel, copper, bronze, lead, wood, leather, paper, wool, cotton, linen, plaster, stone, concrete, or mortar are.** These are legend-words to her — names she has heard in old Skylander rhymes or seen in pre-Apocalypse tablets. She has never touched any of them. Her narrative cannot reach for them as comparisons or descriptors. When the prose needs to describe a hard violet crystal that functions like steel, it says "violet crystal" or "dark-crystal" or simply "the gun-frame" — never "behaves like steel", never "looks like brass". The corollary is that **all material-substitution comparisons (e.g. "as hard as iron", "the colour of brass", "smooth as marble") are forbidden in Kyra's prose** until she crosses the Barrier and learns the words. Per `voice-profile.md §Kyra` and `polish-targets §25` the substitution rule is the same as for nature metaphors: replace with materials Kyra actually has referents for — indigo, deep violet, lighter purple, milky lavender, dark-crystal, the hardness of a Powerstone facing, the brittleness of cheap-grade casing, the sheen of polished gorget plate.

A colossal floating crystal monolith suspended on the Great Geyser — the spire of magical fluid that sprayed from the ocean floor and held the entire city aloft. The Skylands were the homeland of the Skylander people from before the Apocalypse onward. By book 1 the city's population is about two and a half million.

The monolith had multiple distinct strata:

- **The Surface** — the open-air top of the city, where the rich lived among multi-storey crystal buildings. Bathed in daylight; protected by the Great Barrier (a magical shield enveloping the whole city).
- **The Undercity** — the lower levels and tunnels beneath the surface. Where the poor lived. Fungus farms in the deeper levels.
- **The Bowels** — the deepest tunnels; where the worst was hidden.

The city was sustained entirely by magic — water, light, transportation (the Skyway tram running beneath the monolith on a ley line arc), food (fungus farms; no animals could survive on the Skylands). Meat was such a luxury that the poor were sometimes butchered after they could no longer work (Kyra's father met this fate).

### Mana dispensers — scarce, function-specific

Per `magic/system.md` "Skylands mana economy" the city is mana-rationed. **Dispensers are not ubiquitous.** A dispenser is a piece of metered infrastructure that pulls mana off an artificial ley line and throws a low ambient field across a room — like a small radiator of magical energy. They are installed only in rooms with a specific functional need:

- **Mage workshops, training rooms, and casting halls** — where mages cast spells repeatedly and need ambient mana to draw from cheaply (without burning through their personal Powerstones for routine work).
- **Rooms with heavy use of mobile enchanted items** that the operator does not want to keep charging via Powerstone — workshops, manufactory subprocesses, certain Constabulary armouries.
- **Specific industrial processes that require an ambient mana field as part of the production process** — see Stardust-curing under the cartel-operations corollary below.
- **High-end residences of practicing mages and the very rich**, as a household amenity (premium-priced; rare).

The household dispenser in Kyra's apartment is for the gauntlet, the Powerstone-cradle, and the few enchanted appliances she keeps; it is NOT a default Skylander household fitting. Most Undercity households have no dispenser at all and rely entirely on portable Powerstones charged at public stations. Most rooms in any building on the city have no dispenser. When the prose encounters a dispenser, it is doing specific work and the prose should treat the dispenser as evidence of what is happening in that room.

### Cartel operations corollary (Stardust manufacture)

Stardust is manufactured in two stages: **cooking** (chemical synthesis of the crystalline precursor — no mana cost, just chemistry, no dispenser needed in the cooking-rig hall) and **curing** (the cooked precursor is bathed in a thick ambient mana field for hours, which gives the finished product its magical potency and addictive kick). The curing room is the dispenser room: it has a standard mana dispenser AND, in the larger illicit operations, an illegal ley-line tap that boosts the local ambient field well beyond what the dispenser alone would provide. The curing room is the operation's most valuable space — it holds the in-progress product, the finished product, the dispenser-and-tap infrastructure, and the most experienced workers. The cartel guards it accordingly: this is the room where the off-duty Guard with bayonet kits and the cartel-employed mage are stationed.

Workers who spend their shifts in the curing room are visibly affected by chronic exposure to the ambient mana field. The skin takes on a faint violet tinge in the capillaries near the surface; the eyes haze with a thin purple film; the mental affect flattens (chronic ambient exposure dulls executive function the way long-term Stardust use does, since the curing field is essentially low-grade ambient-Stardust-soup). Cartel cant for these workers is *baked* — they are walking advertisements for the product they are curing, and the cartel does not let them leave because their flat affect makes them docile and their bodies are evidence.

**Ecology**: materialised-magic crystal (the substrate of every wall, floor, ceiling, building, tool, weapon, and projectile in the city), fungus (the only living non-human matter on the Skylands; sustains all human nutrition; "spore-bloom" is the only "bloom" Skylanders know), and humans. **No flora and no fauna of any other kind exist on the Skylands and have not for the ~six centuries since the city was founded.** Plants, animals, soil, weather, rivers, and the sea (which is visible from the Barrier but never touched) are six-centuries-ago legend to born-Skylanders. The narrative-voice consequence is documented in `voice/voice-profile.md` §Kyra and `polish-targets.md §25`: born-Skylander narrators cannot reach for nature as metaphor, comparison, or imagery while still on the Skylands. The vocabulary enters gradually once they leave, and the narration softens with it.

**Material absences that follow from the ecology**:

- **No paper.** Paper requires plant fibre. There is no plant fibre on the Skylands. Writing surfaces are etched crystal slate, dried fungal sheet, or (for the rich) cured human leather. No paperwork, no wallpaper, no newspapers, no scrolls, no books in the bound-paper sense. The Skylanders have a written language; they keep records on crystal tablets.
- **No wood.** Furniture, doors, weapon stocks, beams — all materialised-magic crystal, brass, or (rarely) bone. The word "wood" exists in old Skylander rhymes; born-Skylanders do not have a physical referent for it.
- **No leather (animal).** What the Skylanders call leather is **human leather** — tanned skin from the dead, or (in the manufactories) from those still alive but no longer useful. The poor butchered for meat are also tanned. This is not euphemised in Skylander speech.
- **No textiles from plants or animals.** Cloth is woven fungal fibre, gauze made from spun spore-silk, or synthetic material extruded from the manufactories. Wool, cotton, linen, silk are legend-words.
- **No birds inside the Barrier.** The Great Barrier — the magical dome enclosing the city — is impermeable to anything that hasn't been keyed through it. Birds bounce off (when they encounter it at all). Skylanders see birds rarely and from a distance, as silhouettes against the Barrier surface; they have stories about them but no specimens, no close observation, no birdsong. The word "bird" carries the same legend-weight as "tree".
- **Daylight is filtered through the Barrier.** Skylanders never feel direct sun on skin; the dome shapes and softens everything. The first time Kyra steps off the Skylands and the sun hits her unfiltered is a voice event.
- **No metals other than crystal.** No brass, no iron, no copper, no bronze. The Skylands has no fuel for foundries and no ore — every "metal-looking" object in Skylander gear (gun frames, fittings, latches, sword guards, hinges, brackets, sockets) is materialised-magic crystal coloured to look like metal, or a thin synthetic plating over a crystal core. Born-Skylanders do not have a referent for the words "brass" or "iron" any more than they do for "wood" or "tree" — these are legend-words.
- **No plaster, no concrete, no mortar, no stone.** Walls, floors, ceilings, brackets, statues, pavement — all materialised-magic crystal grown out of the fount's outgrowth. The Skylands has no quarries, no kilns, no clay. Born-Skylanders do not have a referent for "plaster" or "stone" beyond legend.

**Mana economy**: documented in `magic/system.md` "Skylands mana economy". Strictly rationed; most Geyser output consumed by city-growth and city-lift; remaining mana flows via artificial ley lines to metered dispensers that fill personal containment fields (Powerstones) and building cores. **No thick ambient mana field anywhere on the Skylands except in the immediate Bowels around the Geyser itself.** Mana is expensive. The scarcity is the load-bearing driver of the book-1 conflict.

The Great Barrier was an enchanted dome surrounding the entire monolith. It was kept impermeable for centuries; only after the Dragonkin invasion (book-1 ch.4) was a portal added to permit Pioneer/Second Expedition traffic.

Destroyed in book-1 ch.51: Kyra Zorantis, in liche-form alongside Malkareth, dove into the Great Geyser and drank it until the column was thin enough to no longer support the monolith. The city tipped, fell, and crashed into the ocean. Two and a half million dead.

## Geography

A monolith of dark crystal — purple/indigo coloured — far out in the ocean. The Great Geyser sprays from the ocean floor; the magical fluid contains and supports the monolith. The Skyway tram exits the city below and arcs along the magic stream.

## Inhabitants (in book 1, before the Fall)

About 2.5 million Skylanders. Hierarchical society: rich Mages and Constabulary leadership at the Surface; mercenaries, dock workers, factory hands, mages-in-training, and farmers in the Undercity; the most desperate in the Bowels. The Constabulary and the Guard ran the city's law enforcement under a corrupt leadership. Stardust drug cartels operated as parallel power. Detective Ford ran an off-the-books anti-Stardust unit using mercenaries.

Notable inhabitants from book 1: Kyra Zorantis, her mother, Greg, Marissa (as a child), Kendall (childhood best friend of Kyra), Markus and his Mage lover (killed book-1 ch.3), Raynard (mercenary; killed book-1 ch.17), Anton (mercenary; killed book-1 ch.1), Detective Ford (Constabulary), Mama June (brothel madam), Alissa (mage on the Pioneer; left in book-1 ch.13). The Pioneer carried the first scouting expedition outside the Barrier; the Second Expedition (which Kendall joined in book-1 ch.36) carried colonists who founded Utopeia.

## Function in the story

The origin of the conflict. Where Marissa was born; where Greg died; where Kendall and Kyra grew up. Sunk by Kyra at the end of book 1; Marissa builds her primary workshop in the same air-space in book 3.

## Notes

- The Skylanders did not know "the Outside" existed until the Dragonkin invasion in book-1 ch.4 breached the Barrier.
- Marissa was born here. She ends the trilogy still referring to herself as "the Last Skylander."
- The phrase "Skylander" became an ideological label rather than a place-of-birth identifier — book-3 ch.4 has Marissa describing the Skylanders' moral and cultural traits as their defining property, not their origin.
- The Great Geyser still flows in book 3. Marissa harvests its power for her workshop.
