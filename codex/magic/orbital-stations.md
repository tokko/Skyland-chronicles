# Orbital Stations

## What it is

Magical satellites placed in geosynchronous orbit around the world, designed to support Marissa's vision of a global magic-distribution grid. Each station is a constructed-magic platform capable of receiving power from ground-based ley lines, storing it, and re-transmitting it to other points on the surface via long-range ley-line beams.

By book-3 ch.11 at least two stations are operational. Marissa launched them from her primary workshop using meta-magic rocket-engine designs derived from her pre-Apocalypse bunker.

## How they work

- **Power reception**: each station has a receiver dish that can accept a long-range artificial ley line beam from the Great Geyser (or from any sufficiently powerful surface fount).
- **Storage**: vast crystal substrate stores magical power as a flywheel against demand peaks.
- **Re-transmission**: outgoing artificial ley line beams point to wherever the station is currently aimed at.
- **Construction beacons**: the stations can drop a "beacon" — a constructed-magic pod — to any point on the surface; the beacon receives power and begins building a fortress, factory, or other facility (book-3 ch.10 — Epitome drops a fortress-seed at the Ca'el front from a Skyship; the same logic supports station-to-ground deployment).

## Marissa's grand plan

The fully-built orbital network would deliver magic to any point on the surface, removing scarcity of building materials, food, water, anything that magic can materialise. The control problem — who decides what is built where, for whom — is exactly the problem Epitome was designed to solve.

The Reliquary of Souls (housed in Epitome) and the orbital network are two halves of the same plan: provide unlimited resources, and provide a non-human moral arbiter to prevent the worst abuses of unlimited power.

## Constraints

- The communication link between ground and station can be intercepted; Marissa has not solved the security problem in book 3.
- Building more stations requires the workshop's main power core (the Great Geyser) — when the workshop is destroyed in book 3 ch.18, station-building stops.
- Stations are still vulnerable to direct attack — but no one in book 3's world has the technology to reach orbit, so the practical risk is low.

## Notes

- The pre-Apocalypse bunker (within Marissa's primary workshop) is the source of all knowledge needed to build orbital stations — Marissa derived "GPS", "satellites", and "geosynchronous orbit" from the ancient machines' written labels and offhand references in their texts.
- Epitome's surviving form is on the most recently-launched station; Barengar's belief at the end of book 3 ch.18 is that he has destroyed her, but the orbital station preserved her.
