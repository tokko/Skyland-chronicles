# Magic System Overview

## The Skylands mana economy (book 1, pre-Fall) — the trilogy's economic spine

**Mana on the Skylands is strictly rationed.** This is the load-bearing fact of book 1's setting and the driver of the whole conflict.

The city's only mana source is the Great Geyser — the column of magical fluid that sprays from the ocean floor and holds the entire monolith aloft. **Most of the Geyser's output is consumed by the city itself**: growing/maintaining the crystal substrate (every wall, floor, ceiling, building, weapon, tool, projectile, gauntlet, and Powerstone in the city is materialised-magic crystal that needs continuous mana to remain manifested), and keeping the monolith floating above the ocean (the city would drop into the sea within hours if the lift-mana failed).

What's left over — a fraction of the Geyser's output — is the **resident mana supply**. It flows through artificial ley lines carved into the monolith's substrate, terminates at metered **mana dispensers** in residential and commercial buildings, and fills the wearer's or building's **local containment field** (Powerstones for individuals; building cores for businesses). Powerstones are the standard portable containment field — they hold a limited charge that depletes with use, and are refilled at the home dispenser (or, at premium prices, at a public charging post). Mana usage is metered and billed. **Mana is expensive.**

The city has outgrown the Geyser. Population growth + crystal-growth demand has compressed the resident ration year over year. The Stardust trade, the Robin Hood mana-thefts, the Constabulary's increasing brutality on illegal mana taps, the recent failures in Kyra's apartment dispenser (the cold water comes hot when the mana comes cold) — these are all downstream of the same scarcity. By book 1 the scarcity is at the level where it has driven the Pioneer Expedition: the city is looking beyond the Barrier for new sources of supply. Utopeia is founded on a mainland fount because the Skylands cannot afford itself anymore.

**Implications for the prose**:

- A Skylander practitioner's personal Powerstone is finite and expensive. Every shot, every spell, every minor manifestation draws from it. The wearer is constantly aware of the reading.
- There is **no thick ambient mana field** on the Skylands (with the exception of the immediate vicinity of the Great Geyser itself, in the Bowels, where civilians do not go). The rationing has made ambient density a luxury — the Surface has thinner ambient than the Undercity (because the Surface is farther from the Geyser channels); the Undercity is thinner than the deepest fungus-farm levels; the Bowels around the Geyser are the only place with truly dense field.
- **Mainland is a revelation.** When Skylanders cross the Barrier they enter a world with natural ambient mana everywhere — ley lines that no one has metered, founts that no one has fortified, magic in the soil. This becomes a discovery for Kyra in mid-book and a strategic temptation for the Skylander leadership.
- Materialised-magic items (revolver casings, glow globes, gauntlets, Powerstones themselves) pull their regeneration / sustenance from whatever local containment field is in range — typically the wearer's Powerstone. Off-Skylands, the wearer will eventually discover that items can also pull from natural ambient. But this is a discovery, not a default.

## Rules

Magic in this world is rooted in Soul — the internal reservoir of magical power a practitioner carries. It is drawn from the practitioner's own Soul to cast spells. External power can be drawn from ley lines (natural or artificial), founts, stored crystals, or — dangerously — from other people.

**Magesense**: The ability to perceive magic. Range, fidelity, and effort vary by practitioner and (for Kyra specifically) by life-stage. Marissa can block others' magesense within a range. Varl is described as having exceptional range.

### Kyra's magesense — two distinct life-stages

Kyra's magesense changes fundamentally after she duels Grasax and acquires his dagger in source-book-1 ch.26-31. The pre-Grasax and post-Grasax registers must be held separate in the prose.

**Pre-Grasax (source-book-1 chs.1-25 approx.)** — **active, deliberate, narrow, qualitative**.
- Kyra has to **deliberately reach for** her magesense to use it. The verbs in the source are "I found my magesense" (ch.4), "I focused my magical sense on it" (ch.19), "I expanded my senses into the lock" (ch.22). It is not always-on; it is a tool she picks up.
- **Line-of-sight-ish range.** She can study magical structures across a courtyard or a forest edge, but only if she focuses; the act of focusing is itself a beat in the prose. People next to her (Anjali pre-training) cannot see what she sees because magesense is a trained skill, not a passive sense everyone has.
- **Detects magic only.** Not bodies. Not heartbeats. Not heat signatures. Not people behind walls (unless they are casting or wearing active magic). She can sense a fount, a shield, an active spell, a soul inside a body (when the body is in front of her), the power-draw of a dispenser, the structure of a lock-enchantment. She cannot sense a sleeping man in the next room.
- **Qualitative fidelity.** She can identify the *kind* of magic (fount vs. capacitor stone, semi-permeable barrier vs. impenetrable shield, a spell mid-cast vs. an inert enchantment), and the *amount* of power involved, with effort. She cannot read the *content* of someone else's spell or thoughts. Coarse passive directional awareness exists — even when her senses are magically dampened she can "see" the crowd behind her at a faint qualitative level — but this is thin, not a substitute for active use.
- **Voice-event implications.** In Kyra's POV (book 1 chs.1-25) the prose uses magesense as a deliberate action with a verb: she *reaches for* the field, she *focuses on* the lock, she *expands her senses into* the spell. The pre-Grasax register avoids "I sensed..." as a passive narrator-tag; she has to do something to know something.

**Post-Grasax (source-book-1 chs.26+ via the dagger)** — **passive always-on, panoptic, requires effort to NOT see**.
- The dagger's enchantment magnifies her magesense to the point where she has to actively *suppress* the input to function. Source ch.23-25 has her watching repair-work across an entire castle simultaneously, seeing bread-bakers in the kitchens and ley-line crossings under the ground, and having to learn to "shift my sight" to one part at a time. By ch.25 she is comfortable with the new baseline. This is the magesense she carries into books 2-3 as a shade and that Marissa inherits awareness of via the gem.
- **Voice-event for the transition**: the first chapter after Grasax-dagger-acquisition should land the upgrade as a sensory voice-shift — Kyra overwhelmed by new input, then learning to throttle it. The reader feels the change in narration mode.

**Powerstone**: see Personal Powerstones below.

### Personal magical shields — two distinct forms

Every trained Skylander Mage carries **two distinct personal-shield mechanisms**, which the prose must hold separately. They serve different threat models and cost the practitioner differently. A third form, the ad-hoc cast directional pane that a mage without gauntlet gear can throw up by hand-shaping, is documented under "Ad-hoc cast shields" below.

#### Form A — the subcutaneous personal shield (universal trained-mage baseline)

A thin magical barrier sitting **just beneath the skin**, integrated into the body. Carried by every trained Mage as a baseline self-defense. Not a device; not a deliberate-deployment cast; it is a low-level continuous integration into the practitioner's own soul-field, maintained passively at a small steady mana drain.

**What it protects against**:
- **Direct-magic-on-organs attacks**. The "boring insta-kills" of magical combat: stopping a heart from across the room, filling someone's lungs with water from the inside, inducing an embolism or a stroke, vapourising a section of brain tissue, reversing peristalsis until the gut bursts. A trained mage attacking another trained mage cannot reach for these as a default opener — the subcutaneous shield catches the casting at the skin-line and dissipates it before it touches an organ. The attacker has to do real work to overcome it (a high-mana sustained attack, a precision-shaped piercing spell, finding a gap when the subcutaneous is drained or down).
- This is the reason mage-on-mage combat in the source defaults to **external force projection** (Anjali's spear of fire, the violet ribbons of force, the explosive-impulse spells) rather than direct organ-attack — the latter doesn't reliably work against another trained mage, and a mage who tries it and fails has wasted a casting.

**What it does NOT protect against**:
- Physical projectiles. Bullets pass through it cleanly; it does not register as a barrier against kinetic matter. A crystal round to the chest of a mage with full subcutaneous shield active will still kill them. (This is why Kyra's crystal-shot kills cartel mages reliably; the mage's subcutaneous catches no metal.)
- Blades. Same as above. Bayonets, swords, daggers all go through it without resistance.
- Blunt trauma. A crowbar to the temple kills a mage as cleanly as it kills anyone.
- Indirect magical effects via the environment (a focused beam that ignites the air around the mage and burns them; a structural-collapse spell that drops a ceiling on them). The shield protects against magic targeting the body directly, not against magic that uses the world as the weapon.

**Cost**:
- Low steady passive drain on the mage's own soul. Negligible at rest; mildly tiring on a long day; comparable to the cost of carrying any maintained spell.
- A practitioner can voluntarily drop the subcutaneous shield to save mana when they are certain no magical attack is coming — for sleep, for long travel, for high-mana casting where every drop counts. Dropping it is a deliberate act. Picking it back up costs a moment of focus.

**Visible signature**:
- None to the naked eye. Visible on magesense as a thin field-glow at the practitioner's skin-line, the same colour as their soulfire (crimson for Kyra; voice-coded by character). Another mage with magesense can read the shield's state — up, down, drained — at line-of-sight.

**Voice-event implications**:
- The subcutaneous shield is in the background of every Mage scene. It is rarely on-page named because it is the default. When the prose reaches for it, it is because something has tested it: another mage tried an organ-attack and was caught, or Kyra had to drop it to spend the mana on a heavy cast, or the shield is leaking after a long fight.
- The Pioneer-expedition era Kyra has this shield as a working professional, picked up during her Guard training under Micah. She does not narrate carrying it; she narrates the moments it does work or fails to.

#### Form B — the expanded egg / sphere (subcutaneous shield deployed at full radius)

The subcutaneous shield can be **expanded outward** into a hardened egg-shaped or spherical barrier around the body, at a radius of one to two feet from the skin. In this form it absorbs **both force and magic** — bullets, blades, blunt strikes, casting, fire, blast fronts.

**Cost**:
- **Severe.** The expanded form is the practitioner's emergency last-resort defence, not a default. Sustaining the egg for more than a few seconds at full hardness can drain a practitioner's reserves to the brink of unconsciousness. Most Mages cannot hold the egg through more than two or three heavy impacts before their reserves give out and the shield drops.
- Powerstone-fed gear (gorget Powerstones, dedicated capacitor stones) can extend the duration, but the egg drains stones at orders of magnitude faster than the gauntlet directional pane.

**Voice-event implications**:
- The egg is reserved for the moment a Mage is bracketed (multiple incoming, no time to use the directional pane), or facing a single overwhelming threat (an exploding manufactorium, a dragon's breath at close range), or absolutely committed to surviving the next five seconds at any cost. It is the spell that empties a practitioner.
- A Mage in the egg cannot cast offensively at full power — the egg consumes the casting bandwidth. A practitioner who has just dropped from the egg is fatigued, slow, sometimes briefly unconscious.

#### Form C — the gauntlet shield (Kyra-specific gear, Kendall-built)

A separate mechanism from the subcutaneous: a **device** worn on the left gauntlet that projects a directional translucent pane in front of the practitioner. Fed off the gorget Powerstone, not the practitioner's soul reserves.

**Deployment**: Kyra raises her left arm and engages the gauntlet; the shield manifests in front of her, defaults to a directional pane projected forward. She dismisses it by lowering the arm or dropping the gauntlet's engagement. The shield is visible to her and to opponents — translucent, slightly distorting, audibly humming when struck.

**What it stops reliably**:
- Bullets (muzzle-loaders, smoothbores, Skylander crystal-shot).
- Shrapnel and crystal fragments.
- Blast fronts (she can wrap the shield into an **airtight bubble** to seal out a fireball or explosive backwash, suffocating the flame on the inside of the bubble).
- Blade strikes at close range (the shield holds; the attacker's blade may snap on the shield surface depending on grade).

**What it stops less reliably**:
- Direct magical attacks. A Dragonkin's "crackling static" arcs across her armour rather than being stopped clean. Against a focused beam she has to **angle the shield to glance the ray off**, not absorb it. Magic-on-magic interactions with the shield are unpredictable and the prose should treat them as variable.

**Shape and configuration**:
- Default: directional pane projected forward from the gauntlet.
- Can be reshaped to: an airtight bubble around her body; a directional pane extended sideways to cover allies pressed up against her; a low-angle slant to glance incoming attacks off rather than absorb them.
- Configuration is fast (one breath) but requires her attention; she cannot maintain a complex shield-shape and cast offensively at full power simultaneously.

**Cost**:
- Drains the gorget Powerstone at a steady rate while sustained. Heavy hits accelerate the drain. When the Powerstone runs low, the shield falls back to her own Soul reserves — fast drain, fatigue, eventually unconsciousness or death.
- She can cast offensively while shielded but at compounded drain. The prudent thing is to drop the shield between magical exchanges.

**Voice-event implications**:
- Pre-Anjali Kyra deploys the gauntlet shield deliberately and economically. She does not run it as a default. She raises the gauntlet when the situation demands it (an enemy mage casting, a charging line of muzzle-loaders, an explosive incoming) and dismisses it the moment the threat passes. This is a working merc's relationship to expensive kit, not a default-protective-bubble of an over-cautious character.
- The gauntlet shield is a Kendall-built device. Every time the prose references it, the Kendall-tagged-tool register from scene-01 carries forward (the gauntlet, the revolver grip, the shield-breaker — all Kendall's work).

#### Form D — ad-hoc cast directional shields (no gauntlet gear)

A trained Mage without gauntlet gear can throw up a directional shield by hand-shaping the cast — one or two hands shaping a pane in front of them, fed off ambient if available or off their own Soul. This is the cartel mage's mechanism in scene-02 (her shield is "a tighter, more directional pane than mine, held one-handed, fed off the room's ambient rather than her own stone").

**Cost**: Personal Soul or ambient, depending on what the mage can reach. Tighter and more directional than Kyra's gauntlet pane because the mage is hand-shaping it precisely; smaller area covered.

**Voice-event implications**: This is the default for Bizhjerai shamans, untrained-but-soulful magic users, and any Mage without specialised gear. The cast pane drops the moment the mage stops focusing on it.

### Why the distinction matters

The subcutaneous baseline (Form A) is why mage-on-mage combat is interesting at all in this world — the cheap insta-kills don't work. The expanded egg (Form B) is the last-resort spell that empties a mage's reserves; the prose can use it as a stakes-raise. The gauntlet (Form C) is Kyra's specific advantage; she carries Kendall's device in addition to her trained subcutaneous shield. The ad-hoc cast (Form D) is the default for any mage without gauntlet gear. The four are not interchangeable; the prose must hold them separate.

When a mage in the prose is mage-attacked at the body (heart-stop, lung-fill), the prose handles it via Form A. When a mage is hit by external force (a bullet, a blast, a force-ribbon), the prose handles it via Form C if Kyra is on-page, Form D otherwise, or Form B as last-resort. The egg appears at most a handful of times across the trilogy and only at moments where the stakes earn it.

**Power cores / storage crystals**: Physical objects that store magical energy. Can be embedded in the body (Marissa has crystals in her breast implants), carried externally, or built into constructions. Can be engineered to detonate explosively (explosive power cores).

**Ley lines**: Natural channels of magical power running through the earth. They emerge at founts. Marissa's innovation is the artificial ley line — a channel she constructs between two points to redirect and supply magic to a settlement.

**Ley-line crossings**: Points where ley lines intersect. Marissa alone knows how to open/create a crossing. This is her key operational security.

**Focusing towers**: Physical structures that receive and redistribute artificial ley-line power across a settlement. The central shield tower in the Groundlands is the nexus for all artificial ley lines and the main dome shield.

**Wild Magic**: Uncontrolled magical energy that forms when a nexus fails and the power spills freely. Comparable to the phenomenon at the "Great Geyser." Spreads and destabilises the environment. The Groundlands generates Wild Magic after the tower explosion.

## Cost

Drawing on one's own Soul to cast spells reduces physical reserves — Blood Magic accelerates this to consuming muscle and fat (visible body mass loss). Overextension causes loss of consciousness or death. Gifting power to another can kill the giver if they push past their limit (as Valia does in chapter 22).

## Limits

Magic can be absorbed by savages — any active spell entering their area of effect is drained. Physical consequences of magic that have already occurred (fire that has already ignited material) cannot be undone by absorption. Magic shields degrade under sustained assault. Very powerful mages can drain spells cast at them (Varl's Mage blocking Marissa's attacks).

## Practitioners

Construction Mages are the elite tier — they build infrastructure, weapons, and complex spell systems. Marissa and Kendall are the two most skilled seen. Mathias Varl is the most experienced.

All Bizhjerai have the Rune, which provides physical enhancement when activated but is not a full magic practice.

Some characters learn magic mid-manuscript: Zandor and Valia were both learning under Marissa's supervision.

## Visible signature

Magic use glows or crackles — violet is associated with Kyra's spirit and Marissa's constructs. Lightning is Kendall's signature. The Bizhjerai Rune blazes when activated. Shield spells create visible shimmer. Artificial ley lines appear as overhead streams of light.

## Notes

- The magic field density of an area determines what is possible — the thick field after the tower explosion enabled Kyra to manifest longer than usual.
- The hierarchy implied: founts > natural ley lines > artificial ley lines > stored crystals > practitioner's own Soul.
