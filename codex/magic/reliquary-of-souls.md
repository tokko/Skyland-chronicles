# Reliquary of Souls

## What it is

Marissa's most sensitive book-3 invention: a magical device housed within Epitome's chest that can extract a human soul from a liche-gem (or, theoretically, directly from a dying body) and integrate its core values, memories, and emotional patterns into Epitome's spell-substrate. The absorbed soul does not remain a distinct consciousness — it is digested, becoming a permanent part of Epitome.

Developed by Marissa across decades using meta-magic; based on:

- **Grasax's dagger enchantment** (book 1) — the liche-binding spell.
- **Kyra's own liche transition** (book 1 ch.50) — proof that a soul can be re-bound to a gem after death and remain coherent.
- **Marissa's own meta-magic spell-finder** — the iterative spell-design technology that allowed her to refine the absorption process beyond what either Grasax or Kyra could have constructed.

## How it works

A magical chamber within Epitome's primary substrate. When a liche-gem (or a dying body, in the case of book 3 ch.18) is placed inside, the Reliquary draws the soul through the dagger's-rune-pattern circuit, decomposes it into memory streams and value-cores, and integrates those into Epitome's substrate. The original soul-pattern dissolves.

Two souls have been integrated by book 3 epilogue:

1. **Kyra Zorantis** (voluntarily, book 3 ch.15-17) — gave Epitome her moral substance, particularly her capacity for rage and loyalty to the Bizhjerai. "Now and forever" — Kyra's last private words to Epitome before integration.
2. **Marissa Zorantis** (book 3 ch.18) — Epitome retrieved her liche-gem from her chest after Barengar killed her, and absorbed her. Marissa's values — patience, abstract idealism, regard for human life — temper Epitome's Kyra-derived pragmatism.

## Constraints

- Requires substantial power to operate; the workshop's main power core was Epitome's principal supply.
- Cannot retrieve a soul that has fully evaporated into the magic field (book 1 ch.49 — Anjali's soul slipped beyond Kyra's reach in the moments after her death; the same window exists here).
- The absorbed soul is consumed, not preserved separately. Epitome describes Marissa as "becoming me, and I would become her" — explicit dissolution.

## Theological/moral implications

In Epitome's book-3 epilogue she considers building a magic-based afterlife — using the Reliquary mechanism in reverse, theoretically, to construct artificial experiential continuity for any soul that has been absorbed. She also considers building "eternal torment" for the wicked. The trilogy closes on this open question — what Epitome will choose to do.

## Notes

- The Reliquary is the trilogy's most morally charged invention: it makes immortality, judgement, and eternal punishment all magically realisable.
- It is housed in Epitome's main avatar's chest by book 3 ch.15; when that avatar is killed in book 3 ch.18, the Reliquary survives long enough to extract Marissa's soul, after which Epitome transmits a compressed backup of herself (with the Reliquary contents) to the orbital station.
