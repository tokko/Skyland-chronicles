# Materialised Magic

## Rules

The core Construction Mage discipline: converting magical energy into physical matter. Used to grow buildings out of the ground (crystal brick structures), create weapons, shape armour, form temporary objects (tarps, rope, splints), or build complex spell-laced infrastructure. Objects created persist after the caster stops concentrating — they are real physical matter, not illusions.

Marissa grew her workshop as "one piece of materialised magic" — a seamless structure she knows intimately. She also grew the fake wall panel that Alissa used to hide the poison jug.

## Cost

Power proportional to the scale and complexity of the object. Large-scale construction (walls, focusing towers) requires ley-line access. Small objects (a tarp, a rope) take personal power. The manufactorium spell — a self-sustaining loop that produces ammunition — degrades with continuous use.

## Limits

The practitioner must know what they are building — materialised magic reflects the caster's knowledge. Errors in design are permanent once cast (Varl's Mage could not solve the thermal equalisation equations for the water-purification spell; Marissa saw the flaw but did not point it out). Objects can be dissolved by the caster or with sufficient magical counter-force.

## Practitioners

Construction Mages: Marissa, Kendall, Mathias Varl, Archibald (lead architect — traditional builder, not necessarily a full Construction Mage). Varl's apprentices.

## Visible signature

The material appears to grow out of nothing. Crystal structures have the distinctive appearance of magic-grown material versus quarried stone. The Groundlands' buildings are all identifiably materialised-magic construction.

## Skylander firearms (book-1 era)

All Skylander firearms — revolvers, muzzle-loaders, repeaters — fire **crystal shot only**. There is no lead, iron, or non-materialised ammunition on the Skylands; the magic substrate of the city is too dense for non-crystal projectiles to behave consistently. The bullet itself is a small materialised-magic crystal slug; the casing is an enchanted shell that regenerates a new slug between shots.

Firing is mechanical: the powder charge propels the crystal slug down the barrel. **The magical cost is the regeneration of the slug inside the casing after the shot.** The casing's enchantment pulls power from one of two sources, in order of preference:

1. **Ambient magical field** — on the Skylands, where the field is dense (sustained by the Great Geyser's outflow), the casings regenerate almost entirely from ambient mana. Sustained fire by a competent merc in Skylander airspace barely touches their personal Powerstone.
2. **Personal Powerstone** — when ambient is too thin (deep tunnels in the Bowels; off-Skylands; inside heavily warded rooms that consume the local field), the casings fall back to the wearer's Powerstone. This is the regime under which Skylanders run dry on the mainland and discover, in book-1's mid-act, how badly their kit calibrates for low-field environments.

A "reload" in Skylander parlance is therefore not the loading of fresh rounds (the casings are perpetual) but the swapping of a worn or fouled casing-set — typically done at home, not in the field. In a firefight a merc may "feed" their gun by holding the cylinder closer to their gorget Powerstone to accelerate regeneration when ambient is poor.
