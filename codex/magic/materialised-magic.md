# Materialised Magic

## Rules

The core Construction Mage discipline: converting magical energy into physical matter. Used to grow buildings out of the ground (crystal brick structures), create weapons, shape armour, form temporary objects (tarps, rope, splints), or build complex spell-laced infrastructure. Objects created persist after the caster stops concentrating — they are real physical matter, not illusions.

Marissa grew her workshop as "one piece of materialised magic" — a seamless structure she knows intimately. She also grew the fake wall panel that Alissa used to hide the poison jug.

## Cost

Power proportional to the scale and complexity of the object. Large-scale construction (walls, focusing towers) requires ley-line access. Small objects (a tarp, a rope) take personal power. The manufactorium spell — a self-sustaining loop that produces ammunition — degrades with continuous use.

## Limits

The practitioner must know what they are building — materialised magic reflects the caster's knowledge. Errors in design are permanent once cast (Varl's Mage could not solve the thermal equalisation equations for the water-purification spell; Marissa saw the flaw but did not point it out). Objects can be dissolved by the caster or with sufficient magical counter-force.

## Practitioners

Construction Mages: Marissa, Kendall, Mathias Varl, Archibald (lead architect — traditional builder, not necessarily a full Construction Mage). Varl's apprentices.

## Visible signature

The material appears to grow out of nothing. Crystal structures have the distinctive appearance of magic-grown material versus quarried stone. The Groundlands' buildings are all identifiably materialised-magic construction.

## Skylander firearms (book-1 era)

All Skylander firearms fire **crystal shot only**. There is no lead, iron, brass, or non-materialised ammunition on the Skylands. The magic substrate of the city is too dense for non-crystal projectiles to behave consistently, and the Skylands has no foundries for non-crystal metals anyway — every "metal-looking" object in Skylander gear (gun frames, fittings, latches, sword guards, hinges, ramrods, bolts, hammers, triggers) is materialised-magic crystal coloured to look like metal, or a thin synthetic plating over a crystal core. The projectile is a small materialised-magic crystal piece in the shape suited to the weapon's bore (round for smoothbores, conical for rifled barrels).

### Discharge mechanism (universal across all Skylander firearms)

Skylander firearms do not use Earth-style black powder. There is no saltpetre, no charcoal, no sulfur, no flint, no match, no wheel. The discharge is magitech across all weapon classes:

1. **Propellant — materialised-mana granules.** Small grains of materialised mana, each one storing a small charge of magical energy. The granules sit in the chamber behind the crystal shot (in the breech for breech-loaders / bolt-actions / revolvers, packed down by ramrod from the muzzle for muzzle-loaders). The granules are inert until the spell-lock fires.
2. **Ignition — the spell-lock.** Built into the gun's lock mechanism, a small mana cell holds a measured charge that the user has set by maintenance at home. When the hammer falls, its impact triggers the spell-lock to discharge its stored mana into the granule chamber.
3. **Combustion — rapid mana sublimation.** The discharge causes the granules to sublimate near-instantly, releasing all of their stored mana into the chamber. The result is a brief, intense, locally dense mana field inside the barrel — a controlled detonation by mana density rather than by chemical pressure.
4. **Acceleration — barrel enchantment.** Every Skylander firearm barrel carries a mass-produced enchantment that converts the local mana field's pressure-equivalent into kinetic acceleration on the crystal shot. The shot leaves the muzzle at speeds comparable to Earth firearms, but the mechanism is purely magitech.

The whole sequence runs in milliseconds and from the shooter's side it feels and sounds like a firearm: the report, the muzzle flash (mana-violet, not orange), the recoil. The smoke is faint — a brief purple wisp from the granule sublimation — and dissipates within a second; there is no Earth-style chemical fog. Granule cost is a real consumable cost on the household ledger: a heavy contract burns granules the same way it burns casing-recharge.

The **spell-lock** must be charged at home off the household dispenser. A standard spell-lock holds enough mana for several days of firing in normal use, or one heavy engagement in extremis. A spent spell-lock is dead until recharged; the weapon becomes a club. Cartels keep their cheaper firearms with under-maintained spell-locks; the misfire rate is one of the reasons cartel armed-labour rounds sometimes do not go off.

### Firearm classes

The Skylands era roughly maps to early-modern through late-19th-century Earth small-arms development, with all classes coexisting in the same market.

**Basic / mass-produced (cartel armed-labour, low-grade enforcer kit):**

- **Smoothbore musket** (muzzle-loader, long-arm). Single-shot. The cheapest and slowest. Loading sequence: half-cock the hammer, pour measured mana granules from a granule horn or pre-rolled cartridge down the muzzle, set a round crystal shot on top, draw the ramrod from its sleeve under the barrel, ram the shot home onto the granules, slide the ramrod back, full-cock, aim, fire. A trained user can manage two or three rounds per minute under pressure. After firing the shooter is committed to a multi-second reload cycle, all of it defenceless. The ramrod is non-optional: a musket without its ramrod is a club. A dead musketeer is identifiable by the ramrod still in the barrel or half-out of the sleeve in his hand.
- **Smoothbore pistol** (muzzle-loader, single-shot sidearm). Same mechanism as the musket but shorter barrel and shorter ramrod. Cartel enforcer-grade sidearm; cheap.

**Mid-tier (Skylander Guard standard issue, mercenary class):**

- **Breech-loading rifle** (single-shot). The breech opens by lever or break-action, the spent casing is extracted, a new cartridge (a self-contained casing holding granules and shot in one unit) is inserted, the breech closes, the hammer cocks, fire. Vastly faster than a muzzle-loader. No ramrod cycle. **Standard Guard-issue rifle.** Most corrupted off-duty Guard carry one of these or a top-hatch variant.
- **Top-hatch-loader** (single-shot). A falling-block or rolling-block variant of the breech-loader: a hinge at the top of the receiver opens upward, the spent casing is extracted, a new cartridge is dropped in, the hatch closes. Slightly slower than the lever-breech but mechanically simpler and more durable. Carried by Guard and senior cartel enforcers who can pay for Guard-tier kit.
- **Repeating revolver** (cartridge-loaded, multi-chamber cylinder). Custom-fitted enchanted casings hold the granules and the crystal shot together. Firing rotates the cylinder for the next chamber. Reloading uses speed loaders for the first cycle, then manual one-by-one from a belt-pouch. Kyra's primary is a 5-chamber heavy-frame on her right hip; her backup is a 6-chamber lighter-frame in the cross-draw on her left.

**Elite (Spire Wardens, top-grade mercs, the rare and the rich):**

- **Bolt-action rifle with limited magazine** (3–5 rounds typical). The bolt is cycled to extract the spent casing and chamber the next round from the magazine. Limited magazine means a reload after a few rounds, but the per-shot rate is faster than any other long-arm in service. **Top-tier kit; Spire Warden standard issue and elite-merc carry.** The Guard does not issue these; they are reserved for the special-forces tier. A bolt-action on the street is a sign of an elite-grade shooter — corrupted Guard do not carry them.
- **Top-grade revolvers** with mana-augmented cycling (a Spire Warden variant of the standard revolver that cycles by sympathetic mana-discharge rather than by hammer-throw, reducing recoil and quickening the cycle). Not on the open market.

**Rare / specialist:**

- **Rifled musket** (muzzle-loader with rifling). Same loading mechanism as the smoothbore but the bore is rifled and the projectile is a hollow-base conical crystal slug that expands into the rifling on firing. More accurate at range, the same load time as a smoothbore. Sniper / hunter kit, not standard.

### Spire Wardens — Skylander special forces

The **Spire Wardens** are the Skylander Guard's elite tactical arm. Selected from the Guard at the top of the rated table, trained for counter-magic operations, hostage extraction, breach-and-clear at workshop-density mana fields, and intercept of mages operating outside Guard sanction. They carry the only bolt-action rifles in routine Skylander service, top-grade mana-cycled revolvers, full materialised-magic plate (heavier and more articulated than standard Guard-plate), and personal gauntlet shields rated to the same Form-C envelope as Kyra's. They do not deploy in numbers; a Spire Warden engagement is one or two operators at the front of a Guard squad, not a unit-on-unit fight.

Kyra is **ex-Guard, not ex-Warden** — she trained under Micah's command in the conventional Guard and washed out before the Warden selection cycle (her own choice; she took the merc trade because it paid). Her gear is Guard-tier with Kendall-custom enhancements; it is not Warden kit. She knows what a Warden looks like and how to read one in a room. If a Warden ever shows up against her in this trilogy, it is a different kind of fight.

The Wardens are not on-page in book-1 ch.1; the cartel does not contract Wardens, and the Guard has not sanctioned this raid (Ford is running it off-book). Their existence in the codex is a tier-marker for the firearm hierarchy and a downstream payoff if any scene puts a Warden in front of Kyra.

### Loadout by tier — at-a-sight identification

The prose must hold the threat-tier distinction at sight:

- **Smoothbore musket** in the hands of a man without armour → labour-tier. Basic threat. One shot before a ramrod cycle.
- **Smoothbore musket** with chopping sword on the belt → cartel musketeer-enforcer. Still labour-tier; the secondary weapon is for after the musket has fired.
- **Breech-loader or top-hatch-loader** in the hands of a man in armour → Guard or ex-Guard. Trained fighter. Faster cycle than a musket.
- **Bolt-action rifle**, heavy plate, gauntlet shield → Spire Warden. Walk the other direction unless paid to do otherwise.
- **Revolver** (heavy frame, mana-cell primer visible at the chamber) → merc-class or Guard. The grip-fit and the size of the cylinder tell the tier.

### Reloading the revolver

Speed loaders for the first cycle, then manual one-by-one from a belt-pouch. Spent casings are kept — the casing's granule chamber and the spell-lock's primer-link enchantment are custom-fitted to the weapon and not freely replaceable. When a merc reloads, the spent casings go into a hip-pouch or coat pocket, not on the floor. At home, the spent casings are dropped into a recharge cradle that connects to the household mana dispenser; the cradle refills the granule chamber and re-seats a fresh crystal slug inside the casing over several hours. A merc who throws away spent casings is a merc not coming home with rent money.

### Standard mercenary loadout (book-1 Kyra)

- **Primary repeating revolver**: five-chamber cylinder; Kendall-built; grip fitted to Kyra's hand; spell-lock primer. Worn at the right hip. Speed loader holds five.
- **Backup repeating revolver**: six-chamber cylinder; lighter frame; spell-lock primer. Cross-draw on the left hip. Speed loader holds six.
- **One belt-pouch of loose casings** for manual reload after both speed loaders are empty.
- **One hip-pouch / coat pocket for spent casings**, taken home and recharged.

A merc's fast-cycle engagement budget is therefore: 5 + 5 (primary + its speed loader) + 6 + 6 (backup + its speed loader) = **22 fast-cycle shots**, plus loose casings loaded one at a time after that. Most engagements end well before twenty-two. A merc who runs the speed loaders dry is having a bad day.

### Cartel and underworld arms (book-1 era)

The Stardust cartels equip their staff in tiers. From cheapest to most expensive:

- **Antechamber cheap labour**: smoothbore musket and a knife or short club. Recruited off the street; not trained in the weapon. Standard cartel doorman / antechamber kit. Spell-locks under-maintained; misfires are common.
- **Cooking-floor and rig-hall guards**: a mix of smoothbore muskets and short blade-swords of the cheaper grade, the kind that snap on crystal if you hit wrong. Slightly more reliable spell-locks than the antechamber tier, still armed labourers rather than soldiers.
- **Curing-room musketeer-enforcers**: smoothbore musket plus a short chopping sword (falchion-style) or a hatchet or a heavy club at the hip for when the musket has been fired and the ramrod cycle is too slow. **They do not have bayonets** — the cartel does not equip its men with fixed-blade muskets. The musket is a single-shot ranged piece; once it has fired, the secondary weapon comes out. Armed labourers and enforcers, not soldiers and not fencers.
- **Corrupted off-duty Skylander Guard**: Guard-issue plate, **breech-loading or top-hatch-loading carbine** (Guard kit, never a muzzle-loader and never a bolt-action — bolt-actions are Spire-Warden tier, not standard Guard issue), short sword on the belt. Trained fighters — not soldiers, but they know what they are doing in a fight in a way the musketeer-enforcers do not. Distinguishable on sight by the plate, the modern long-arm, and the way they move.
- **Cartel mage**: a Construction Mage of varying tier. The senior rooms (curing room, vault) carry one. A real defender.

The prose must hold the distinction between **armed enforcer** (cheap labour with a muzzle-loader) and **trained Guard** (corrupted but skilled, with a bolt-action or breech-loader). They are different threats.

### Ford as contractor (not a faction)

**Detective Ford runs no faction.** He is an individual contractor. For a job like the book-1 ch.1 raid he hires multiple separate mercs and small teams under separate contracts — four breach-points on the same block in ch.1, each hired separately. The mercs know each other by reputation and by name, not by faction membership. Prose shorthand like "Ford-contracted mercs" or "Ford's mercs" must not read as a faction identifier: write "mercs Ford had hired" or "the other hires off the same contract" or use the individual mercs' names. There is no permanent unit, no insignia, no shared loyalty. Each merc is paid per job and goes home.
