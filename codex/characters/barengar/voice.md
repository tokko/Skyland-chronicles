# Voice — Barengar

## Register

Direct, plain, slightly stiff. Provincial Bizhjerai-tinged Skylander Common. Reads younger than he is — careful where Marissa is bawdy, deferential where Marissa is profane. Softens under stress rather than getting brittle.

When he is the POV he has more interior reflection than Marissa: he asks himself questions, he second-guesses his impulses, he notices things he then dismisses. His narration is closer in close-third than Kyra's first-person was — more thinking, less editorialising.

## Vocabulary

- College/Mage register: "enchantment", "rune", "magesense", "shield", "ley line", "channel". Picks up Marissa's idiosyncratic terms — "meta-magic", "spell-finder", "construction" — and uses them carefully, like quoted phrases at first.
- Bizhjerai cant inherited from his upbringing: "the Clan", "Spirits", "our people". Less fluent in it than the warrior-Bizhjerai (Jani, Marie, Anjali) — he is a Mage, not a warrior, even though he carries an axe.
- "deoch draoidheil" — the Bizhjerai-tongue name for the savages. He uses it consistently; the Skylander/Marissa side just says "savages".
- Domains for analogies: woodworking, hunting, mathematics. He almost never reaches for sea-faring or city imagery.
- Profanity: sparing. "Fuck" comes out of him under real pressure — taunting the savage in ch.2, swearing at the Archangel's spell density in ch.3. Never as filler.

## Syntax

Medium-length sentences with a slight tendency to declarative chains. Asks himself rhetorical questions in narration ("Was it… No, it couldn't be?"). Under stress his sentences shorten; under boredom they lengthen. Notably, his narration uses contractions ("I'd", "it's") much more than Marissa's, which is part of his younger register.

## Tics

- Internal sigh-narration: "This was going to take a long time, and it was going to be supremely boring."
- "Got it" / "Understood" as default acknowledgement to Marissa — never "Yes, lady" or anything formal.
- Calls Marissa by name (not by title) once she insists on it in ch.2 — "I'm no lady".
- Calls Kendall "old Kendall" or "Kendall the Builder" in narration; never met him directly on-page through book 3.
- A throat-clearing politeness when approaching Marissa — clears his throat before announcing himself in her workshop.
- Notices women's physical features in narration (Jani's curves, Marissa's ass in tight trousers) and then immediately catches himself with "she was old enough to be my mother." This self-aware-male-gaze beat is a signature of his POV.

## What he avoids saying

- His own family / origins beyond "I came from the east of the Bizhjerai lands". The source never gives his parents' names, his clan, or his pre-College history.
- Any open assessment of Marissa's plan in dialog with her — he listens, asks follow-ups, internalises.
- Romantic statements to Jani in dialog; their attraction is in body language and narration, not declared.
- Anything insulting about the Skylanders. He has been raised on the legend of Marissa, the destroyer of the Groundlands; he treats the Skylander past with quiet horror, not contempt.

## Examples

```
"Come one then, bitch!"
— book-3 ch.2; taunting the wounded female savage as she charges. The rare moment of bared profanity. Short, percussive, unguarded.

"I couldn't be arsed to do all of those pipes by hand, so I automated it."
— book-3 ch.3; the smug-apprentice tone. Note the British "couldn't be arsed", which is part of Barengar's register and never Marissa's.

"That is not possible. There must be thousands of spells in this thing."
— book-3 ch.3 (the Archangel); the Mage-techical-disbelief beat.

"I think that I respected her more than anyone in this world."
— book-3 ch.18 (after killing Marissa); the only sentimental statement he allows himself.

"Got it, Marissa. I will sit back and try to enjoy the ride."
— book-3 ch.3; the dry-acknowledgement default.
```

## Anti-examples

- "By the Spirits, what is this thing?!" — too declarative; Barengar would simply ask "What is this thing?"
- "She was magnificent." — too florid; Barengar admires through specifics, never through abstractions.
- "I, too, was a Mage" — formal inverted phrasing alien to him.
- Any "I felt a sense of..." formulation — Barengar's POV either names the body-fact ("my palms were suddenly sweaty") or the inference ("I was bored"), never the abstract emotional category.
