# Voice — Kendall

## Register

Cultured, precise, warmly arrogant. He speaks like someone who has mastered his field and knows it — but does not weaponise the knowledge. Commands are crisp ("Fire at will, whenever the gunners believe is the opportune moment"). Personal speech is affectionate and slightly formal.

## Vocabulary

Naval command language; architectural vocabulary; the polite vocabulary of someone educated in the Skylands' upper levels. Uses "young one," "child," "kiddo" for Marissa. Uses "old friend" for Kyra. The word "excellent" appears when he is pleased.

## Syntax

Medium-length sentences with clean subordination. He does not trail off. Under stress on the bridge he shortens: "Report." He never uses profanity in direct speech on the page — he lets his tone carry weight instead.

## Tics

"Be silent, child" — his affectionate dismissal of Marissa's (correct) criticism. "Excellent" — approval marker. "You'll see" — when setting up a plan he knows will work. "I thought I would find you here" — how he opens the epilogue scene.

## What they avoid saying

His grief. He swallowed his sorrow over the deaths of Skylands residents, of Bizhjerai crew, of what victory costs. He does not discuss this. He cries once (chapter 28 farewell) and clears his throat immediately.

## Examples

```
"I'm not a man prone to violence but I have already asked you politely once. Now get off my ship you motherfuckers!" — ch26; the contrast between the formal first clause and the profanity is the whole joke; it reveals he is at the absolute end of his patience.

"Perhaps you will learn something." — ch27, teaching mode; gentle condescension, genuine affection underneath.

"Keep us turning around, I want to face her head on!" — pure command voice; short, directional, certain.

"I thought that I would find you here." — ch28; understated; he has known her long enough to know where she goes when she needs to think.
```

## Anti-examples

```
"I feel deeply conflicted about the choices we made today." — too named; he processes conflict through action and decision, not statement.

"The loss of those people weighs heavily on my heart." — he would never sentence-structure his grief this way; he would refocus on what comes next.
```
