# Alana

## Identity

Headmistress of Marissa's College of Magic in book 3. About a decade older than Marissa; lacks Marissa's degree of Blood-Magic age-arrest and shows it — her hair has thicker streaks of grey, "the faint wrinkling of a distinguished woman". An old friend of Marissa's, longer than she has been employed by her, and a remarkably fit woman for her age. Calls Marissa "old friend" and is the one person at the College who can casually demand Marissa do something Marissa does not want to do.

She runs the College's day-to-day operations while Marissa retreats into her workshops to research Epitome and the orbital project. Her style of management contrasts with Marissa's — Alana is the diplomat, Marissa is the killer. ("You probably would have a much larger cleanup crew. Your style of diplomacy is… Let's just say that it is different from mine.")

She runs the College through and after Marissa's death — book 3 ch.18 makes clear that her stewardship will continue after the events of the final battle.

## Physical

Tall (book-3 ch.3 implies she is taller than Marissa). Grey-streaked hair. Lined face. Carries herself well; sprints down towers with even breathing. Wears Skylander-style office attire.

## Voice

Wry, diplomatic, "clipped tones" on the speaking-tube. Calls Marissa "Marissa" — never "Lady". Drops light barbs at Marissa's preferred methods. No formal `voice.md` yet — distinct from Marissa, distinguishable from Marie, but only a small handful of lines in the source.

## Goals

**Surface goal**: Keep the College running and growing.

**Deeper goal**: Maintain the practical legacy of Marissa's work after Marissa is gone.

## Notes

- Alana is **not** the same character as **Alissa** in book 2 (slug: alissa). The names are similar but the characters are distinct: Alissa died in book-2 ch.20; Alana enters in book 3 ch.3.
- Alana's relationship to Marissa is the closest the trilogy has to a long-running female friendship in Marissa's adult life. Note: she does **not** know about Epitome, the workshop, or the orbital project — Marissa has compartmentalised her.
