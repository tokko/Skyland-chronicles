# Anton

## Identity

A large Skylander mercenary hired alongside Kyra by Detective Ford for the opening raid on the Stardust drug-manufacturing site in book-1 ch.1. He is heavily muscled in a way that "could not have been entirely natural" — the result of an **artificial berserker graft**, a Skylander surgical-magical procedure that strips donor muscle from cadavers (or, in the cheaper underworld variant of the procedure, from the still-living poor who have run out of other things to sell) and threads it into the recipient's own muscle bed alongside woven layers of materialised-magic fibre that hold the borrowed tissue alive on a slow trickle of mana. The graft delivers explosive strength and a chemical flood of aggression on demand. It runs hot under sustained load and can fail catastrophically (burnout, seizure, cardiac arrest, the donor tissue rejecting back into necrotic rot) when the recipient pushes past the graft's thermal envelope. The seam-scars across major muscle groups are diagnostic — graft-mercs carry visible surgical lines along the collarbones, shoulders, and inner arms where the dead-meat strips meet their own. Anton fights with a crowbar in one hand and a single-shot muzzle loader in the other.

**On the graft's effect on the recipient — the rule the prose must hold.** The graft is a *berserker enhancement*, not a possessing spirit. The grafted soldier remains the actor at all times; the graft floods them with aggression, bloodlust, and explosive strength, and inhibits the higher-order judgement that would normally restrain them, but the swing of the crowbar is still the recipient's swing, the choice of target is still the recipient's choice, the cruelty is the recipient's cruelty. The graft does not "take over" or "drive" the body; it loads the recipient to the point where they will kill anything in the room until the chemistry burns off. A grafted soldier in full graft-flood is best understood as the recipient with their conscience and forethought chemically suppressed, not as a separate entity inhabiting the body. The prose must never grammatically treat the graft as the subject of action verbs (do not write "the graft turned him toward the women" or "the graft was on her before five"; write "Anton turned to the women" or "Anton was on her before five steps, the graft-flood doing the targeting before his conscious mind caught up"). Anton is on the page as a man whose cruelty has been chemically amplified, not as a body being puppeted from inside.

He is overcome by berserker bloodlust during the raid — the known and indeed deliberate effect of the graft once it's been driven hard enough to flood the system — and abandons his agreed partner-role with Kyra (she was to lead, he to cover) to slaughter everyone in his path, including non-combatant Stardust cooks. Kyra, jaded by years of Skylander work and unsurprised by what graft-mercs do once the rage lights up, does not intervene; she revises her tactics on the fly and uses him as a distraction. Anton is shot in the back of the head by a Stardust guard's musket in the manor's grand hall and dies instantly.

He is a function character — his role is to establish the world of book-1 ch.1 (Skylander mercenary culture, the Stardust drug war, the Skylands surface architecture) and to demonstrate Kyra's combat improvisation. He has no arc.

## Physical

Tall, broad-shouldered, "twice as wide" as Kyra. Bulging muscles beneath a sleeveless vest. Carries a crowbar and a single-shot pistol.

## Voice

Single line of dialog only (book-1 ch.1 — howls of bloodlust, no real words). No `voice.md` warranted.

## Goals

**Surface goal**: Kill Stardust dealers; collect mercenary pay.

**Deeper goal**: Bloodlust.

## Notes

- Anton's body is found in the manor hall. Kyra leaves him with the other dead.
- The artificial berserker graft is a hint that the Skylands has a sub-market in surgical-magical body modification beyond Stardust itself, and that the trade in cadaver and live-donor tissue is one of the city's quiet horrors. Grafts are expensive, illegal in the Guard, visible (seam-scars at the collarbones and shoulders; running hot — steam off the skin in cold rooms, visible flush when the graft engages), and require continuous low-grade mana from the recipient's Powerstone to keep the borrowed tissue alive. A graft-merc with a dead Powerstone has, within hours, a flesh-bed of necrotic strips inside their own muscle.
- The graft's failure modes (burnout, system collapse, donor-tissue rejection into rot) are part of why graft-mercs tend to die young.

## Graft-mercs in the cartel and underworld economy

The berserker graft has a clear market niche. Cartels and Skylander enforcer outfits keep one or two grafted men on the books not because they are good *soldiers* — they are not — but because the reputation does the work the soldiers don't have to. A man known to lose control once the chemistry is up, known to kill cooks and cleaners and customers and his own backup once the graft floods, is the kind of man whose presence in the room makes everyone in the room compliant. The fear of triggering him is the lever. Most of the time the graft never engages; the threat that it might is enough. This is the cartel's preferred labour: a walking guarantee that defiance ends in slaughter.

Once the graft does engage, the grafted soldier is unreliable as a tactical asset. They cannot be redirected mid-flood. They will not stop on a command. They cannot be trusted around hostages, the rest of the team, or specific named targets — the chemistry does not allow discrimination, and the soldier in graft-flood does not have the cognitive bandwidth to enforce a kill-list. Useful as a front-line shock asset where the brief is *slaughter and destruction* — break a defensive line, clear a barricaded room, terrorise a hostage population into surrender — and useless for anything requiring restraint, precision, or surgical violence. Kyra (book-1 ch.1) is the foil: she is the surgical professional, hired for the precise opening kill and the high-value target; Anton is the meat-grinder hired to make the rest of the manufactory's defenders fold in on themselves before Kyra reaches them. Ford pairs them because the contract has both jobs in it.

The reputation feedback loop is part of why the graft persists in the trade. Each public slaughter is advertising. Cartels lean on the rumour-network deliberately: the next manufactory's defenders have heard what happened in the last one, and many will run rather than engage. The grafted soldier's own value scales with the body count, which means a graft-merc has an economic incentive to do the very thing the graft was always going to do. The system rewards what it punishes.
