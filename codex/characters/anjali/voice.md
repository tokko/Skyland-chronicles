# Voice — Anjali

## Register

Direct, declarative, plains-bred. Shorter sentences than Kyra. Often dry-witted. Becomes harder, sharper, more profane after her father's death — but never affected.

## Vocabulary

Bizhjerai cant: "the Clan", "our people", "Outcasts", "Clanless", "the Spirits". Battle and animal-husbandry vocabulary native; engineering vocabulary halting at first, gradually fluent through book 1 as Kyra teaches her.

Picks up Skylander profanity from Kyra — "fuck", "shit", "asshole" — but uses it more sparingly than Kyra and almost never as filler. When Anjali swears, she means it.

Domains for analogies: hunting, weather, livestock. Almost never uses Skylander-side metaphors (cogs, wheels, manufactories) — she leaves those to Kyra and Kendall.

## Syntax

Sentence length skews short and medium. Simple SVO. Interrupts herself rarely; finishes thoughts cleanly. Under stress her grammar gets shorter and her body language louder — she touches Kyra's arm, makes eye contact, repeats names.

## Tics

- Calls Kyra by name (not by pet name) most of the time. Only Kyra calls her "Red"; Anjali never returns the gesture.
- Calls Marissa "little one" once she is fully integrated as their daughter.
- "Now and forever" — said only twice in the source, both times to Kyra, both times life-defining: their marriage (book-1 ch.36) and her death (book-1 ch.49).
- Says "Spirits" (in the Bizhjerai sense) where a Skylander would say "Gods."
- Practical questions when overwhelmed: "Why?", "How does that help?"
- Slight teasing edge — pokes fun at Kyra's worse traits (stubbornness, vanity, sex jokes) but never with malice.

## What she avoids saying

- Her own mother's name (her mother is dead and grieved off-page).
- Her father's name, even after his death — calls him "father" or "the Chief".
- Anything that would directly call Kyra a Skylander after their marriage — she elides the word.
- Will not call Marissa anything but "Marissa" or "little one" — never "Mary" even when Marissa later goes by that alias.

## Examples

```
"I've known since I saw your Bizhjerai Rune. When I tucked you into bed after you exhausted yourself on the plains. I've known a long time who you are, and loved you anyway. You are a wonderful person Marissa. I don't hold your upbringing against you. You're with us now, that's all that matters."
— book-2 ch.22 (echoed from book-1 dialog patterns); the longest sustained run of dialog in this voice — note the short declarative sentences, the absence of profanity, the gentle weight.

"Then why have it up in the first place? Wouldn't that just waste power?"
— book-1 ch.19; the practical-question tic. Short. Pressing for the load-bearing point.

"Now and forever."
— book-1 ch.49 (last words); the family phrase.

"Sytrok."
— book-1 ch.15; a single Bizhjerai word to release Kyra from spell-fetters during the Outcast raid. Cool under fire.

"Calm yourself, Red — that's me. The Red Terror." (paraphrased from book-1 ch.49 banter)
— rare moment of self-mocking the nickname her enemies gave her.
```

## Anti-examples

- "Time to fight. Time to die." — too portentous; Anjali would just go.
- "It wasn't just a battle — it was a reckoning." — negative-parallelism is alien to her cadence; she would say "It was a slaughter" and leave it.
- "Anyway..." — Anjali does not throat-clear between thoughts. If she pauses, she pauses physically.
