## Notes for the writer

- This is the book where Arc II's middle has to be redistributed. Polish-targets §8 names the source's worst case (the 13.5k-word "Construction Mage" chapter where Marissa lectures Valia on magic theory). That material is critical — meta-magic, the dagger-vessel mechanic, the intent-vs-outcome parable, the relationship-with-Valia foundation — but it must not be delivered in one chapter. Distribute across the book:
  - The dagger-vessel reveal lands as a private scene between Marissa and the shade (no Valia present).
  - The intent-vs-outcome parable lands as a flashback / dialogue fragment between Marissa and the shade.
  - The meta-magic conception is a single beat at the dam (one short scene, not a lecture).
  - The remaining magic-system content is scattered across teaching moments, workshop interludes, and brief lines of dialogue.
- H-alissa-arrives-in-disguise and H-meet-alissa-as-shadow-ally happen in the same source scene but are functionally two beats. The first is the reveal (Marissa recognises Alana as Alissa); the second is the operational shape of the alliance (Alissa is staying as the invisible second). Stage them as a single scene with two distinct beats, or as two adjacent scenes — the orchestrator's choice during chapter outlining.
- H-the-dagger-is-the-vessel — Marissa works out the mechanic. The reader already knows from Arc I. The scene's tension is therefore not the *reveal* but Marissa's reaction: she has been carrying her mother in a gem on her hip and has not understood it. The grief of having had her mother in arm's reach and not known is the beat.
- H-mother-on-intent-vs-outcome is the trilogy's load-bearing ethical claim. It lands here, in a relatively quiet shade-conversation, and then echoes through every subsequent moral act Marissa commits — the killing of Alissa (Book 5 close), the genocide (Book 6), and Epitome's terminal cold-blooded ethics (Book 9). Land it cleanly. Do not over-decorate.
- H-the-farmers-are-murdered is the book's moral pivot. Apply polish-targets §22 (monologue-over-the-corpse) ruthlessly at the farmstead — Marissa's interior monologue over the dead daughter is one paragraph, not a page.
- H-meta-magic-spell-conception is short, structurally — one conversation at the dam. But its reach is the whole trilogy. Land it with the right weight without inflating the scene.
- H-marissa-shoots-alissa is the book's terminal grief. The execution is on-page; the reader has been allowed to like Alissa for half the book; the act must be brutal. Apply polish-targets §11 in reverse: this beat does not get a body-comedy softener. Polish-targets §22 in reverse: this *is* a corpse Marissa is allowed to stand over. One paragraph.
- H-marissa-survives-the-box is the book's lowest point. The voice in the box should fragment — sentence-length distribution at minimum, fragments as the dominant shape (voice §Marissa cadence under stress). Do not over-explain her madness; show the broken nose against the wall and the missing fingernails.
- AI-ism watch: this book is the source's deepest middle and the source defaults to long block paragraphs (§3 / polish-targets §12). Break aggressively. Establish paragraph-break rhythm by mode-change.
- Working title: *Crucible* is voice-correct without being over-elevated. The book is where Marissa is tested — by love, by recognition, by the box. The plain word is enough.
