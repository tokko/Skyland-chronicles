## What's leapt at the open

A short interval (a season or so) since Book 2 closed. The colony is now consequential — its raiding parties have been crossing into Clan territory, and the Clans have been answering. Book 3 opens on the marketplace reunion at Dun Traelak (source ch.41) as the new equilibrium image: Marissa runs into Kyra's arms in the trade-stalls; Kendall's mother grips Kyra's wrist; the world shrinks to three.

## What's leapt at the close

Roughly a year between the delivery of the memory-gem (Book 3 epilogue) and the start of Book 4 (Marissa, ~22, walking into a Skylander ball in alias). The leap is the entire ~15-year arc of young Marissa being raised by Kendall, Alissa, and Marie in Dun Traelak. The reader does not see the raising; they see its outcome. The hand-off is the gem.

## Notes for the writer

- This book carries the trilogy's first catastrophic grief and the trilogy's first genocide. Both are load-bearing for everything Marissa later does. Neither can be softened.
- H-anjali-dies must come without warning. The book has spent two books proving Anjali survives the wounds that should kill her — Grasax's staff through the gut, the dragon-rider's spear, the Outcast's club. The bullet that kills her is *not* heroic. It is one of thousands fired in a city-scale battle and it happens to find her head while Kyra is carrying her to safety. The reader should not feel a death-arc closing; they should feel a thrown coin landing wrong.
- H-kyra-becomes-bas-marbhadair must keep the source's gallows-dry register. The source has Kyra say "There was no pain. My heart was already shattered to pieces near the shores of the Elwyer Forest, and now it was literally so." That joke is voice-correct under maximum bleakness and must survive the rewrite. See voice §Kyra anchor 5.
- H-kyra-destroys-the-skylands is the trilogy's first genocide. The source's strongest move is the rolling-magesense paragraph in ch.50 (voice §Kyra anchor 6): Kyra sees small intimate moments of life in the doomed city — a toddler taking a first step, a woman in the privy, a baker drawing a tray from an oven — and after each she registers "I pulled harder." Preserve this exactly. Resist sentimentalising the small lives; resist sentimentalising Malkareth's death; let "I pulled harder" do the work.
- H-the-memory-gem-arrives is a fragile beat. It is also the bridge to Arc II. The voice rotates here for the first time — the epilogue is Marissa's POV at age ~13, having just relived Kyra's life. The voice must be recognisably the precursor of Marissa's adult voice without being the same voice. Younger, less ironic, more sincere. The line "I would kill them all" must land as a child's vow, not as a teaser.
- Apply polish-targets §16 (the stomp-the-girl flashback): one final flashback in this book at the moment when Kyra rolls her magesense over the falling Skylands, before the small lives. The book pays the source's repetitions in a single closing beat.
- Apply polish-targets §22 (Marissa's monologue-over-the-corpse): Kyra has the same tic at Anjali's deathbed in the source — pages of retrospective inside a single grief-paragraph. Compress. One paragraph at the actual death; let the cremation and the dagger-suicide carry the rest.
- AI-ism watch: the book's set pieces are large and the source defaults to map-tour establishing shots and tricolon (§3.5, §3.8). Cut on first pass.
- Working title: *The Fall* is plain. The Skylands fall; Kyra falls; the family falls; the world falls into Arc II. The book closes on a memory-gem in a child's slack fingers, and that is the only image the title needs to carry.
