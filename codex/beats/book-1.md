## Notes for the writer

- This is the canary book. The first scene is the trilogy's first ever sample-read for the user; pacing, voice fidelity, and AI-ism suppression all matter twice as much here.
- Kyra's voice runs more diagnostic than Marissa's — see voice §Kyra.distance and §Kyra.cadence. Suppress §Kyra anti-anchor 1 (the unbroken thousand-word fight paragraph in source ch.1): break the opening breach into rhythm units.
- Apply polish-targets §15 (post-battle sex as scene-default): the Raynard scene is voice-correct once but must not become Kyra's default cool-down beat across the book. The killing of Raynard at ch.17 reframes the pattern in a single act; reach that beat without staging two more interchangeable Raynard encounters along the way.
- Apply polish-targets §16 (the stomp-the-girl flashback): one flashback in this book, max two. The first appearance is in ch.2; the second is when Kyra mistakes Anjali for the dead child at ch.11. Cut the rest of the source's recurrences.
- H-the-magic-shortages must thread the whole book — it is not a single scene but an ambient condition. Marissa's apartment dispenser failing, the Robin-Hood gang draining people, the gang violence around Stardust manufacture — all are facets of the same shortage. The reader must understand by ch.7 that this is why the Skylanders will sail.
- H-greg-dies-saving-kyra: the Dragonkin invasion is a major set piece. Resist the source's tendency to render it as a thousand-word chunk of choreography. Break by lighting, location, and adversary.
- Kyra-the-mercenary's voice must be established as load-bearing before Greg dies: the reader needs to believe she will survive, financially and emotionally, the loss of her only friend.
- AI-ism watch: Kyra's lexicon trends toward concrete trade-vocabulary (revolver, Powerstone, gauntlet, shift, deal). When the prose reaches for "delve" / "tapestry" / "intricate", substitute with the precise Skyland trade term.
- Working title: *Stardust* is voice-correct (the drug, the cartel, the substance the city runs on) and survives the book it opens.
