## Notes for the writer

- **Book 6 is the structural pivot of Arc II.** Marissa's plan crystallises here but is not yet executed. The book is the bridge between Books 4–5 (infiltration / cover-keeping) and Book 7 (the genocide itself). The pace shifts from cover-management to sabotage-engineering at exactly the rhythm of the source's ch.21–24.
- The book has two acts:
  - **Recovery and the savage trap** (source ch.21–22): mother-absolution, the red flare, Felaan dies, the egg-chamber, Valia falls. Four hard beats; the book's emotional first act. Two named loved-ones die. The team is broken before the sabotage can begin.
  - **The sabotage is rigged** (source ch.23–24): poison in the water, magazines rigged, the horde driven off the wall. Two hard beats; the book's external climax. The plan is complete; the trigger is not yet pulled.
- H-mother-on-killing-alissa is the book's opening beat. Apply voice §Marissa anchor 6 inflected through book-2 Marissa: the shade speaks plainly, the absolution lands, the box-broken Marissa accepts it. The shade is operational because the magic field around the bedridden Marissa is dense — but the corporeality is muted; the shade is at the edge of presence. Hold the cadence: §Marissa anti-anchor 1 (page-long retrospective dumps) is the worst risk here. Marissa is half-dreaming; the shade speaks; Marissa accepts; the scene ends. Do not over-explain.
- H-valia-falls is the book's deepest beat — and the trilogy's deepest single beat in Arc II. Valia's revelation that she has known Marissa's identity for months and loved her anyway must land cleanly. See voice §Marissa anchor 4: the source's strongest move is to let Valia speak a clean run of sentences without Marissa commentary-stepping on them. The rewrite holds the same restraint. The cliff-fall, the gift of all Valia's power, the breaking-free from Marissa's grip — all sequenced cleanly; no monologue-over-the-corpse (polish-targets §22) on the way down or on the way up.
- H-egg-chamber-as-foreshadow is the visual seed of Arc III's central reveal. The wooden ramps, the rows of nested eggs, the careful carving — the savages built this, lovingly, for their young. The reader and Marissa register the implication ("they are not animals") without naming it. The naming comes in Book 7's epilogue when Kendall hands her the tome.
- H-the-savage-horde-attacks is the book's external climax. Apply polish-targets §3 (action minutiae) hard — the source renders the wall-defence in two source chapters of choreography. Break by lighting (flares, lamps, the morning fog rising), by weapon (artillery cycles, Angel mortar drops, Marissa's blood-magic), and by character moment (Wylah losing her composure when Felaan's name comes up at the wall, Zandor's controlled efficiency that the reader is about to learn was a man in love who knew her identity). The scale is thirty thousand savages dead; the texture is the team's narrowed-down condition.
- H-marissa-poisons-the-water is the first concrete sabotage. Apply voice §Marissa engineering register: the poison goes in the way a Construction Mage installs anything — measured, calm, with the workshop's mind. The covert tail Marissa murders at the wall is one beat, not two; she does not pause to monologue over him.
- AI-ism watch: this book is the worst case for polish-targets §17 (cross-narrator "suddenly") and §3 (action minutiae) in the horde-attack chapters. Strip on first pass.
- Working title: *The Sacrifice* names the book's emotional weight (Valia gives Marissa her life and her power both; Felaan gives his life for Wylah; Marissa accepts the absolution and commits to a plan that will kill thousands). The title is plural; the cost is paid by the team, not by Marissa alone. The trigger of the sabotage waits for Book 7.
