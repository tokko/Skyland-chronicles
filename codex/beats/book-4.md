## What's leapt at the open

Roughly fifteen years between the end of Book 3 (the memory-gem in young Marissa's lap) and the open of Book 4 (Marissa at twenty-two, in alias, in a Skylander-survivor city's ball, cocking her hip at Gereon). The leap is the entire raising-of-Marissa-in-Dun-Traelak — Kendall as guardian, Alissa as surrogate aunt, Marie as foster-matriarch, the Bizhjerai Rune tattooed in her teens, the formal mage-training, the marksmanship, the years of preparation. None of it is on-page. The reader meets a finished operative.

## What's leapt at the close

A short interval (days to weeks) between Kendall's confrontation in the mountains (Book 4's closing beat) and the beginning of the polyamorous configuration (Book 5's opening). The summoning-crystal Kendall has left her sits in her pocket through Book 5 unused.

## Notes for the writer

- Marissa's voice arrives full-formed. The reader has not seen her since the epilogue of Book 3, where she was a thirteen-year-old in a borrowed bedroom. The first scene of Book 4 must make the leap feel earned without explaining it: a working operative in an alias performing innocence in a register the reader did not see her acquire. See voice §Marissa anchor 1 (the kill scene).
- Apply polish-targets §14 (Marissa says "I lied"). The book is structurally a long con; the temptation to flag every lie is maximum. Cut the explicit "I lied" tag on first pass. The reader gets it.
- Apply polish-targets §22 (monologue-over-the-corpse). The source's worst case is here — the page-long backstory dump over Gereon's body in ch.1 (Marissa anti-anchor 1). Compress hard. The kill is one paragraph; the backstory is parcelled across the next four chapters.
- Apply polish-targets §8 (magic-system lecture mode). H-first-ley-line-opened is the temptation. Show Marissa doing the spell, then doing the next thing, then doing the next thing. The magic-system file holds the theory.
- H-mother-shade-first-appears is *now* a mechanic reveal rather than a major reveal, because Arc I has dramatised Kyra's life — the reader knows Kyra became a liche and lives in the dagger. The on-page reveal is therefore the question of *how* Kyra is reaching Marissa, not *who* Kyra is. The shade's first manifestation should still land as a beat for Marissa (she has never seen her mother) — but the reader knows what they are looking at before Marissa does.
- H-uncle-kenny-tries-to-take-her-home is the book's first emotional pivot. Kendall is the man who raised Marissa from thirteen to twenty-two; their fight in the mountains is the first time Marissa has lifted a weapon against him. The shade stepping between them rotates the book's central ethical question into view (intent vs outcome): Kendall would do the same thing Marissa is doing, just faster and bloodier, and the shade refuses to let him.
- Voice watch: Marissa's bawdy register is in full effect across Book 4. Apply polish-targets §11 (sexual / body-comedy as default break) at the trim pass — one body-comedy moment per scene at most.
- Working title: *Infiltrator* is plain and accurate. The book is the con; the alias; the embedding; the first reveals. The job description is the title.
