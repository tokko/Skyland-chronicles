# Hard Beats

The spine. Every beat listed here **must** be reached, on-page, in logical and naturally-motivated fashion. Hard beats may arrive earlier or later than originally outlined. They may not be skipped, off-screened, summarized, or downgraded.

This file is auto-included in every turn so the orchestrator always knows what's still owed to the story.

Beats are listed in narrative-chronological order across the trilogy. IDs use the form `<H|S>-<short-slug>` per `codex/beats/README.md`. Source references point to chapters in the three source manuscripts under `source/`; the 9-book target series will land them in the appropriate target chapters during outlining.

## Format

Each beat:

```
### <BEAT-ID> — <Short title>
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-N / ch.MM
- **Target arc / book**: arc-N / book-M
- **Why hard**: the structural reason it cannot be cut
- **Description**: what occurs, including the felt shift in the POV character
- **Status**: pending | landed (scene: <book-N/ch-MM/scene-PP>)
```

## Arc I — Kyra (source-book-1)

### H-kyra-merc-life — Kyra opens as a working mercenary in the Skylands
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.1
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The trilogy cannot open without the Skylands as a city the reader sees from inside, by someone who has paid the cost of living there. Kyra-the-mercenary is the precondition for everything Marissa will later avenge.
- **Description**: Kyra leads a drug-raid breach party for Detective Ford. She kills efficiently, watches Anton (her enhanced muscle) lose himself in bloodlust, takes her payment, and walks out of the burning Stardust manufactory. The chapter establishes her voice (close, professional, unromantic), her city (the layered, drug-fraught Skylands with its captive economy), and her dependence on Kendall's engineering. The post-raid encounter with Raynard introduces Kyra's body-as-instrument and her own self-loathing about wanting violent men.
- **Status**: pending

### H-kyra-kills-a-child — Kyra accidentally kills a thirteen-year-old runner
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.2
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The single load-bearing moral wound of source-book-1. It hangs over every future kill, and it shapes Kyra's reaction to red hair (the foundation of her mistaking Anjali later). Without this beat, Kyra's voice has no centre.
- **Description**: On a Stardust escort job with Greg, Kyra fires explosive rounds into the dark after a snatch-and-grab. The thief is a small girl with red hair. Kyra reaches her in time only to stomp her face in to end her suffering. She cries; Greg understands; her name is now the kind of name that gets a child killed.
- **Status**: pending

### H-the-magic-shortages — the Skylands' power supply is failing
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.1–3
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The reason Skylanders go to the mainland — the reason there will be a Utopeia, a war, a Fall — is established here as a slow-running emergency. Without this the rest of source-book-1 reads as random adventure.
- **Description**: Kyra's apartment magic-dispenser fails. The water comes hot when the magic comes cold. The "Robin Hoods" (a Stardust-adjacent gang) are tapping mainlines or draining people for their magic. The Skylands' Great Geyser is producing the same magic it always has but the population has grown too large. The crisis that will motivate the Skylander expedition to the mainland is established as economic, not military.
- **Status**: pending

### H-greg-dies-saving-kyra — the death that breaks her in
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.6 (mid-act setpiece)
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The Skylands' invasion is the inciting external event; Greg's death is its personal cost. The chain Kyra→Greg→Marissa-as-orphan is the seed of Marissa's adoption-by-Kendall later.
- **Description**: During a Dragonkin invasion of the Skylands (the first cross-Barrier attack), Kyra rides a wounded dragon to kill its riders; Greg dies in the fall, saving her. The cost of her recklessness is named. Kyra is hospitalised; she carries Marissa out of Greg's empty apartment.
- **Status**: pending

### H-kyra-volunteers-for-the-expedition — leaving the Skylands
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.7–8
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The trilogy's geographic pivot from Skylands to mainland. Establishes the technology that all three books will use, and the relationship (Kyra-Kendall-Marissa) that anchors the family.
- **Description**: After the Dragonkin invasion, the Skylands organise an expedition through the Barrier — volunteers paid to cross over and assess the threat beyond. Kyra signs up. She kisses Marissa goodbye (her newly adopted ward, in care of Kyra's mother and Kendall). Kendall hands her a small bag of equipment that turns out to include the prototype Powerstone, the materialising-armour gauntlet, and the means to materialise weapons. Kyra crosses the Barrier on the airship and the Skylands recedes behind her.
- **Status**: pending

### H-meet-red — Kyra encounters Anjali and mistakes her for the dead child
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.11
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The pet name "Red" is born here and outlives both characters. The mistaken identity is the foundation of Kyra's love and her guilt simultaneously — without this scene the relationship has no shape.
- **Description**: A red-haired Bizhjerai woman captures Kyra at spear-point. Kyra's first glimpse of her — the red hair lit from above — triggers the flashback to the murdered child in the Skylands. The mistake colours every interaction for weeks. Anjali ("Red", as Kyra nicknames her) takes Kyra captive and is assigned her keeper.
- **Status**: pending

### H-kyra-fights-for-the-bizhjerai — the raid against the Outcasts
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.15
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-1
- **Why hard**: The first cross-cultural alliance between Skylander and Clansman. Anjali's first kill is on-page and load-bearing. The Chief becomes Kyra's father-in-law in this scene by implication, even if it takes ten more chapters to formalise.
- **Description**: The Bizhjerai camp is attacked by a rival Clan ("Outcasts"). Kyra, still captive, demands her weapons; Anjali frees her on her oath; together they fight off the flankers. Anjali kills her first man on the same night. The Chief (Anjali's father) acknowledges Kyra's worth and grants her freedom-with-escort.
- **Status**: pending

- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-2
- **Why hard**: The relationship that will end with Anjali's death at Utopeia — every later beat depends on this being a real marriage, on the Mark being a real Mark, on the name being a real name.
- **Description**: Kyra is adopted into Clan Bizhjerai. The night of her adoption she sleeps with Anjali for the first time. The Bizhjerai Rune is tattooed onto her shoulder. The Bizhjerai pet name for her, "Bàs Marbhadair" (Deathbringer / Plague-Death), is given. The marriage is functionally formalised, though Bizhjerai marriage customs differ from Skylander.

### H-grasax-the-liche — first encounter with the Bizhjerai's old enemy
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.26–28
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-2
- **Why hard**: The dagger as artifact is established. Anjali surviving a belly-wound on Grasax's staff is set up for the parallel belly-wound at Utopeia that the reader will fear is fatal (and which initially is not — only the stray bullet afterward is). The lichedom mechanic is named.
- **Description**: Grasax — a Greater Dragon turned liche — attacks the Bizhjerai. Kyra fights him alongside Anjali; she nearly dies; Anjali is impaled on his staff. Kyra kills Grasax (or contains him) and recovers his dagger — the dagger that will hold Anjali's soul, then Kyra's own. The dagger is the trilogy's most important physical artifact.
- **Status**: pending

### H-malkareth-hatches — Kyra and Anjali raise a dragon together
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.31–32
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-2
- **Why hard**: Malkareth is the trilogy's recurring physical-power lever and the emotional anchor of Kyra's late book. His death is the moment Kyra Archons. The bond is established here.
- **Description**: A dragon egg, retrieved from Grasax's stash, hatches in Dun Traelak's fount. Kyra is bonded to it immediately. The dragon is named Malkareth. Kyra and Anjali are now parents in two directions — to the dragon, and to the human child (Marissa, far away in the Skylands, who Kyra has not seen in over a year).

### H-the-first-skylander-incursion — Utopeia is founded on the coast
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.40–42
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The trilogy's central political conflict is named on-page. Utopeia's existence is the cause of the genocide Kyra will commit (against her former home) and of the genocide Marissa will later commit (against Utopeia's survivors). Without this beat the trilogy has no antagonist-of-history.
- **Description**: Skylander settlers arrive on the eastern coast of the mainland — the colony that will become Utopeia. The Skylanders cut Elweyr Forest for lumber, expand into Clan territory, and establish a fortified perimeter. The first trade negotiations between Skylanders and Clans are awkward and clearly temporary; both sides know war is coming.

### H-the-battle-of-dun-traelak — the Skylanders attack the Bizhjerai stronghold
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.44–47
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The book's first major battle and the moment the Clans become a unified war-force. Anjali becomes Chief in her father's name. The Bizhjerai-Dragon alliance is sealed. Cannot be off-screened.
- **Description**: A Skylander expeditionary force assaults Dun Traelak with their Hand of Glory Skyship and ground troops. The Bizhjerai are nearly overwhelmed. The Chief (Anjali's father) dies in the breach. Malkareth and Kyra (now wing-flying in Valkyrie armour) bring the Red Flight of dragons to the battle and destroy the Hand of Glory. The Skylanders are routed. Ten thousand Skylanders die.
- **Status**: pending

### H-the-march-on-utopeia — the Clans go on the offensive
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.48–49
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The trilogy's first city-scale battle. The mechanics of breaking a ley-line-powered shield are established (Marissa will later use the inverse — sabotage from inside — in book 2). Cannot be off-screened.
- **Description**: The unified Clans march on Utopeia. Fifty thousand Clansmen surround the city. The dragons drink the artificial ley line and break the Skylander shield. The Clans assault through the breach. The fighting devastates both sides.
- **Status**: pending

### H-anjali-dies — the stray bullet
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.49
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The book's emotional climax and the trilogy's foundational grief. The phrase "Now and forever" is given its full weight here. Marissa carries this into both her later books. Cannot be off-screened, summarised, or softened.
- **Description**: Anjali is gut-stabbed in the breach but Kyra stabilises her. Carrying her back to the shaman, a stray musket-shot strikes Anjali in the head. She dies in Kyra's arms inside the dragon-shield. Kyra plunges into her dying soul, tries to wrap her, instead consumes her power, and Anjali dissipates with the words "Now and forever".
- **Status**: pending

### H-kyra-becomes-bas-marbhadair — the cremation and the suicide
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.50
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The transformation that produces the mother-shade of books 2 and 3. The dagger is now a vessel for Kyra; the gem in its pommel will outlast her body. The trilogy's central magical mechanic is created here.
- **Description**: At Anjali's pyre, Iolan (rival Chief) sneers and Kyra murders him on the spot. She walks back to her chambers, undresses, holds the dagger between her ribs, and stabs herself through the heart. The dagger's enchantment binds her soul. She wakes a liche.
- **Status**: pending

### H-kyra-destroys-the-skylands — the fall of the city
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.50
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The book's title beat and the trilogy's first genocide. The Fall of the Skylands is the historical pivot the rest of the books react to. Malkareth's death is the cost paid. The memory-gem mechanism is established for book 2's opening.
- **Description**: Kyra flies with Malkareth to the Skylands. She plunges into the Great Geyser and drinks. Malkareth joins her against her wishes and dies consuming more than his body can hold. Kyra Archons. The Geyser thins; the Skylands slides off its perch and falls into the ocean. Two and a half million die. Kyra encodes her memories into a gem and dispatches it via a Bizhjerai-spell-beast to find Marissa.
- **Status**: pending

### H-the-memory-gem-arrives — Marissa relives Kyra's life
- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-1 / ch.51 / Epilogue
- **Target arc / book**: arc-I / book-3
- **Why hard**: The bridge between source-book-1 and source-book-2. Without this beat, book 2 has no foundation. The "thousands escaped" line is the source-book-2 plot in one sentence.
- **Description**: Months later, in Dun Traelak, a spell-beast drops the gem on young Marissa's pillow. She relives Kyra's life in a single night. She knows now: her mom-Anjali died at Utopeia; her mother-Kyra died destroying the Skylands; she is the inheritor of both their names. Kendall arrives, takes the gem, relives it himself. Together they understand. The Skylander threat is not over — thousands escaped into Elweyr Forest.
- **Status**: pending

## Arc II — Marissa (source-book-2)

- **Source book / chapter**: source-book-2 / ch.1
- **Target arc / book**: arc-II / book-4

He volunteers that a new Skylander settlement exists, that he has a map, and that they should travel together. The reader meets a narrator who is performing innocence with surgical contempt — voice and goal established in one go.
