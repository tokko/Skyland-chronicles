# Voice and Beats — Extraction Summary (full trilogy)

Re-extracted across the three source books — `book-1-the-skyland-chronicles.md` (Kyra, 51 ch + epilogue), `book-2-the-last-skylander.md` (Marissa, 28 ch), `book-3-epitome.md` (alternating Marissa/Barengar, 18 ch + prologue + epilogue). Supersedes the prior book-2-only extraction.

## Voice in five bullets per narrator

### Kyra (book 1)

- **First person, close, tactical.** Where Marissa's voice runs a script, Kyra's voice runs a triage — every room ranked by sight lines, every man by his weapons, every wound by minutes-to-die. She editorialises constantly but the editorial is professional rather than performative.
- **The default sentence is medium and comma-spliced; fragments are rare and only fire in grief.** The grief mode is short ("Red was gone."); the working mode is long, accumulative, and run-on. The voice's signature pacing-brake is a single observational sentence that reframes a long action passage.
- **Mercenary register sits on top of a slowly-acquired Clan vocabulary.** The lexicon transition itself is voice — early chapters use Skylands engineering terms, mid-book she is still translating ("the spirits", "the elements"), late book she uses Clan terms without italicisation. The phrase "Now and forever" is born here.
- **Body honest, unromantic, frank.** The post-battle sex scenes, the breasts that have started to sag at twenty-eight, the brutal practical relationship with men. Marissa inherits the register. Where Kyra differs from Marissa is the lack of the bawdy joke — Kyra is not playful about sex; she is matter-of-fact about her appetites and her self-disgust.
- **Crystal and dark crystal are the world's signature substance.** When Kyra leaves the Skylands, the absence of crystal is itself a visual disorientation. Wood smoke smells like burning meat. The fluid-and-glass palette established here travels into books 2 and 3.

### Marissa (book 2 — youthful voice, the previous five bullets, modified)

- **First person, very close, very honest.** Marissa narrates as she calculates — every observed person and surface judged, every choice second-guessed on the page, the contradictions exposed live.
- **The default sentence is medium-to-long; short sentences and fragments are the punchline.** Long sentences in process and engineering thought; short sentences in killing, grief, and reversal.
- **Bawdy, profane, and engineering-precise at the same time.** The same narrator who can describe the topology of a meta-magic spell ("a three-dimensional squiggly line of power channels") will also note "fucked, and not the good kind." Both registers are voice.
- **The world's signature material is crystal and the world's signature sensation is pressure.** Materialised magic surfaces "tinkle"; founts "geyser"; the body "drinks" power; the air "thins" when magic is spent.
- **Mother-tongue and mercenary register sit on top of a small private lexicon: "mother", "mom", "Now and forever", "Cursed be her name", "There are no innocent Skylanders".** These are emotional anchors and the rewrite must use them deliberately — not as filler.

### Marissa (book 3 — older voice, the variant)

- **The voice has cooled.** Bawdy register mostly gone (residual). Engineering register has expanded to structure interior monologue almost completely. The voice is now pedagogical (Marissa has been a teacher for decades). The voice is also more isolated — no lovers, no team, only Barengar-as-apprentice and Kyra-as-shade.
- **Fragment-frequency has dropped, long engineering sentences are more common.** The rewrite should leave a few of the book-2 fragments in older Marissa as voice-echo (especially when Zandor reappears or when she remembers Valia) so the reader hears the same narrator inside the older body.

### Barengar (book 3)

- **First person, close, present-to-the-action, with editorialising one beat late.** Barengar reports the world hunter-style: taxonomy first, structural detail second, the unsettling fact third. The reader watches him being curious without his explaining the curiosity.
- **Sentences are shorter than either Kyra's or Marissa's.** SVO, two-clause compound, frequent fragment-as-observation ("Clever.", "Boring.", "Damn."). Combat prose is the cleanest in the trilogy. The voice's late-book cadence lengthens after the Oath breaks — he has lost the structure and the prose is finding its footing.
- **Native Clan vocabulary on top of a learned Collegium vocabulary.** "Deoch draoidheil" not "savages" except when paraphrasing Marissa — the two-name habit is identity.
- **Profanity is low.** Barengar swears in stress, not in commentary. A clear character marker against Marissa-level density.
- **Body-noticing of Marissa is shy and apologetic** where Marissa would have just looked. Voice-marker for an Oathed apprentice who is also in love.

### The Epitome (book 3 closing frame, ~one chapter)

- **Voice is a composite of three (Epitome + Kyra + Marissa) speaking as one.** The "I" never quite resolves. The voice is patient where the humans were urgent, ethical-philosophical where they were practical. Sentences short, evaluative, near-biblical.

## Source strengths (rewrite priorities to preserve)

1. **Three distinct first-person voices in sequence.** Kyra (book 1), Marissa (books 2 + 3), Barengar (book 3) — each voice is genuinely distinct in cadence, lexicon, and editorialising distance. Add the Epitome epilogue as a fourth, briefly. The trilogy's voice-architecture is its single strongest asset and the rewrite must protect each narrator's separateness; cross-POV bleed is the single most common reviewer-flag risk in book 3.
2. **The magic system is load-bearing, internally consistent, and engineering-real.** Materialised-magic substrates, ley-line founts, focusing/receiver towers, manufactoria, spell-stones, blood magic, meta-magic, the savages' draining aura, and (in book 3) meta-magic intelligence + soul-extraction + orbital communications. The cosmology grows in three books without contradicting itself. The world rewards the reader for noticing.
3. **The moral architecture is honest across all three books.** Kyra is a Skylander who destroys the Skylands; Marissa is a Bizhjerai who destroys Utopeia's daughter colony; Epitome is a meta-magic that promises Utopia and threatens eternal torment. The trilogy never lets any of its protagonists off the hook and refuses to pretend any of the genocides are righteous. The thematic spine ("intent vs outcome" → "Outcome can be good, intent matters little, no one knows in the moment") is the philosophical claim Epitome literalises in the epilogue. Hard, clean, earns its bleakness.
4. **The dagger as artifact is genuinely load-bearing.** Forged by Grasax, captured by Kyra, used in her suicide, carries her soul, found by Marissa, used in book 2's magic-channelling, used in book 3 as a portable shade-companion, ultimately destroyed in the workshop explosion. A single physical object holds the trilogy together. The rewrite must keep it constantly on-page.
5. **The Zorantis family structure is unusual and earned.** Marissa is the adopted daughter of two mothers — Kyra (Skylander, Traitor, mercenary-turned-liche) and Anjali (Bizhjerai, Red Terror, Chief). She is raised after their deaths by Kendall, her gay uncle. Her later polyamorous triangle with Valia and Zandor in book 2 is a generational repeat of her mothers' marriage. The structural symmetry must be preserved.

## Source weaknesses (rewrite priorities to fix)

1. **Pacing collapses in middle book chapters.** Book 2's "Construction Mage" (ch.12, 13.5k words) is mostly Marissa-explaining-to-Valia. Book 1's middle chapters (ch.30–40, life at Dun Traelak) drift into idyllic-time without enough plot tension. Book 3's middle (ch.10–14, the post-Kendall-funeral chapters) loses urgency before the Epitome-spiral. The rewrite must redistribute exposition into scenes that already have plot work, and condense the idyllic and grieving interludes.
2. **Voice has tic-density problems that are author-level across all three narrators.** "Suddenly", "of course", "I sighed/shrugged/nodded", "the fuck" as filler, "boobs" as scene-default, reflexive height-jokes (Marissa) and reflexive body-noticing (Barengar) and reflexive grief-flashback to the dead girl (Kyra). Catalogued in `polish-targets.md` §1, §15–§24.
3. **The author has signature images that recur as reflexes.** Boot-stomping faces (Kyra ch.2, Marissa ch.27, Zandor ch.9 of book 3). Long uninterrupted single-paragraph fight scenes. Post-battle sex (especially in book 1). The author returns to these shapes whether or not the scene needs them. The rewrite must keep one of each per book at most.
4. **Magic-system over-explanation is endemic.** All three narrators slip into lecture-mode (Kyra explains magic to Anjali in book 1 ch.12, Marissa explains it to Valia across book 2, Marissa explains Epitome to Barengar throughout book 3). The dialogue-with-"I'm with you so far" is the universal tell. The rewrite must show the thing once, name it later, never explain.
5. **The mother-shade scenes have a single shape repeated.** Marissa hits a wall → shade appears → shade comforts and absolves → shade fades. Book 3 partially fixes this (mother is sarcastic in the prologue, sharp-tongued about Epitome). The rewrite must vary the shade across the trilogy: sometimes wrong, sometimes sharp, sometimes unable to comfort.

## Resolution of the 7 original tiering questions

The seven tiering questions from the prior book-2-only summary. Of the seven, six are resolved by books 1 and 3; one remains open.

1. **Mother-shade appearances (eight in source).** STILL OPEN — needs user input on which four to keep as hard.

   Sharpened framing: with book 3 in view, the four hard shade scenes should be (a) first manifestation in book 2 ch.6 (the dagger-as-vessel premise), (b) the absolution after killing Alissa in book 2 ch.21 (the only shade scene that has narrative consequence), (c) the prologue of book 3 (the sarcastic working-partner mode that establishes the new relationship), and (d) the shade transferring her magic to Barengar's Angel armour in book 3 ch.9 (the sacrifice that saves Marissa). The other shade appearances — the ledge in book 2 ch.10, the dialogue with Barengar in book 3 ch.6, and the brief appearances elsewhere — can be soft, reshaped, or cut. User to confirm.

2. **Intent-vs-outcome parable.** RESOLVED — promoted to hard. Book 3's Epitome of Morality is the literalisation of the parable; the trilogy's ethical claim is now structural, not optional.

3. **Alissa's arrival.** RESOLVED — promoted to hard. Book 1 establishes Alissa as Kyra's adoptee, a Skylander who chose the Bizhjerai before Marissa was born. Her covert role in book 2 is a generational repeat of Kyra's own crossing.

4. **Zandor surviving.** RESOLVED — promoted to hard. Book 3 confirms Zandor as the Demagogue. His survival is no longer ambiguous; it is load-bearing for the entire book 3 plot.

5. **Savages-as-engineered-precursors as outer frame.** RESOLVED — confirmed as hard. Book 3 opens on the Groundlands taken back by savages, and the deoch draoidheil are Barengar's entrance-exam quarry. The pre-Apocalyptic engineered-humans frame is the trilogy's continuing post-genocide world.

6. **Polyamorous Marissa/Valia/Zandor triangle.** RESOLVED — promoted to hard. Book 1 establishes Kyra/Anjali as a real marriage and Marissa as the daughter of two mothers. The triangle is no longer optional ornament; it is generational repetition. Book 3 confirms the cost: Zandor returns as the Demagogue thirty-five years later, his survival predicated on the depth of the original bond.

7. **Genocide-as-title intensity.** RESOLVED — confirmed at full intensity. Books 1 and 3 each contain their own large-scale killings (Kyra's destruction of the Skylands; Marissa's destruction of Utopeia in book 1; the Ca'el war and Epitome's avatar-fleet in book 3). The trilogy is structurally a triptych of mass-deaths. Softening book 2's intensity would break the trilogy's spine.

## New tiering questions raised by full-corpus view

These are questions that did not exist when only book 2 was visible. The orchestrator should surface these to the user before the per-book beat lists are finalised.

8. **Book 1's first-act sex scenes (Kyra-and-Raynard, etc.).** The source's reflexive post-battle-sex pattern is a polish target (§15 in polish-targets.md). The Kyra-Raynard scene is voice-defining and probably must stay. But the source has at least four similar scenes in the first ten chapters. Is the user comfortable keeping one such scene per ten chapters, or should the rewrite cut more aggressively?

9. **The book-2-to-book-3 time jump.** Book 2 ends with Marissa riding south alone. Book 3 opens six months later with the Groundlands prologue, but the Collegium that anchors book 3 is described as decades old. There is a missing middle (~25-30 years) where Marissa founds the school, raises Epitome's earliest prototypes, and survives without companions. Does the user want this period dramatised (new book-3 opening scenes? interlude chapters? a flashback frame?), or is the prologue-as-leap acceptable?

10. **Headmistress Alana's identity.** Book 3 names the Collegium's headmistress "Alana" — almost certainly the same name Alissa used as her cover in book 2 ("Alana Kelvari"). Is the user confirming that Headmistress Alana is the same Alissa Skylander, somehow alive (faked-death? body-double at the trial?), continuing her covert role into the next generation? Or is Alana a separate character and the name is coincidence? The trilogy's coherence depends on this answer.

11. **Barengar as the trilogy's framing narrator.** Book 3 ends with Barengar taking the memory cores of both Marissa and Kyra and vowing to transcribe their lives into books. This is the trilogy's meta-frame: books 1 and 2 are Barengar's reconstruction. Is the user committing to this framing, in which case book 1 and book 2 should carry subtle signs of being Barengar's reconstruction (occasional voice-bleed, gaps in the record, transcription marks), or is the meta-frame to be dropped and the books treated as omniscient first-person?

12. **The Epitome epilogue tone.** The trilogy ends not on peace but on a calmer iteration of the same problem — Epitome in orbit, planning eternal torment and eternal bliss. Is the user committing to this ending, or does the rewrite have permission to soften it (Epitome falls silent; Epitome is destroyed; Epitome is constrained)? The current source ending is genuinely bleak and the rewrite's market position depends on which way the user wants the trilogy to land.

13. **Malkareth-the-dragon's role across the trilogy.** Malkareth dies at the Fall of the Skylands in book 1. He is referenced as "dragon brother" in book 2 but never appears. He is not mentioned in book 3. Is the dragon-brother's death the only role he plays in books 2 and 3, or should the rewrite plant continuing dragon-kin presence (a Greater Dragon visiting Dun Traelak in book 2, Barengar encountering Drakonids in book 3)? Currently the dragons drop out of the trilogy after book 1 in a way that may feel arbitrary.

14. **The "Now and forever" verbal anchor.** Kyra coins it for Anjali in book 1. Anjali says it as she dies. Kyra echoes it to Malkareth before he dies. Marissa carries it across books 2 and 3 — she nearly says it to Zandor, says it to Valia at the cliff, says it to Kendall when he dies in book 3. Does the user want Barengar to inherit the phrase (say it to Marissa as he buries her? say it to Jani?), making it a four-generation Zorantis lineage marker? Or is the phrase exclusive to Zorantis women?
