# Extracted Beats — `source/manuscript.md`

Every major story beat extracted from the 28-chapter source manuscript, in narrative order. Beat IDs use the form `<H|S>-<short-slug>` per `codex/beats/README.md`. Book assignment is deferred — these beats will be split across books 1–3 after user approval.

This list is the input to classification + tier-approval before any per-book beat lists are produced.

---

### H-cover-seduction — Marissa cons Gereon at the ball
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: At a Skylander gathering in one of the Cities, Marissa (using the alias "Mary Laier") plays a ditsy, harmless Skylander orphan to seduce information out of Gereon, a Skylander she has marked as her first kill. He volunteers that a new Skylander settlement exists, that he has a map, and that they should travel together. The reader meets a narrator who is performing innocence with surgical contempt — voice and goal established in one go.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: This is the inciting scene that introduces voice, premise, alias, and method. The trilogy cannot begin without this exact con-and-target setup; everything downstream depends on Marissa having a map she earned by being underestimated.

### H-first-kill — Marissa executes Gereon and reveals her real identity to the reader
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: On the road, after letting Gereon pull her off her horse and pretend-faint into his hands, Marissa shoots him in the back of the head, takes the map, and reveals via interior monologue that she is Marissa Zorantis — adopted Bizhjerai daughter of the Traitor Kyra and the Red Terror Anjali — on a vow to kill every surviving Skylander, one at a time, starting now.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: This is the book's first reveal and the mission statement. Without this on-page kill the trilogy's premise is undelivered.

### S-bike-on-the-plains — Marissa builds and rides her materialised-magic bike
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: After the kill, Marissa cleans the scene, releases the horses, materialises a magic-engine bicycle from a powerstone, and rides north toward the Skylander settlement. First demonstration that this Skylander is also a Construction Mage at Kendall-tier skill, and that the magic system is industrial.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: The voice + system reveal is great but the specific vehicle and route can be reshaped. Pacing scaffold, not spine.

### H-arrive-at-the-groundlands — Marissa enters the new Skylander settlement
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa rides into the camp that will become "the Groundlands": a long-house, scattered tents, a few hundred survivors. She is challenged at the gate, surrenders her weapons under a fake policy, presents Mathias Varl's stolen tome as proof of training, and is sent to find Micah.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The location that anchors books 1–2 is named; the central deception (Varl as her invented master) is planted. Removing this scene removes the floor under the entire middle of the trilogy.

### H-meet-micah — Marissa meets the leader she will eventually undermine
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa meets Micah, the pink-haired ex–drill sergeant who survived Utopeia. He recounts being burned by Kyra, having trained her in the Guard, and his quiet horror at the Skylands' fall. He invites Marissa to make herself useful, treats her as a problem-solver, and within hours has earmarked her as central to the settlement.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Micah is the trilogy's primary antagonist-of-conscience; this scene establishes both his moral complexity (treats her well) and the conditions under which Marissa will eventually have to kill him.

### S-tree-felling-machine — Marissa's first sabotage-friendly Skylander tool
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa observes the camp's bottleneck (lumber), pulls schematics, and materialises a magical chainsaw-equivalent in front of the men. Micah blesses it. Marissa's plan to embed herself as indispensable is operating.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Establishes infiltration tactic and Construction-Mage register but is one of several possible vectors; could be reshaped.

### S-defence-planning-with-micah — Marissa and Micah design the walls together
- **Source chapter**: 3
- **Description**: Sitting in dirt, Marissa and Micah sketch the future fortification of the valley mouth. He overrules her smooth-curtain wall in favour of jagged salients that overlap fields of fire; she absorbs the lesson. Their working rapport is set up — the relationship that will make her final betrayal cost her something.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Wonderful character work but the specific tactical exchange could be condensed or moved without breaking the spine.

### H-the-team-arrives — Marissa is assigned Zandor, Valia, Wylah, Felaan
- **Source chapter**: 4
- **Description**: Micah assigns four young Skylanders to escort Marissa on the trek to the first ley-line crossing: Zandor (huge, cheerful, will become lover), Valia (golden-haired, shy, will become lover), Wylah and Felaan (the stoic warrior couple). The trilogy's secondary cast enters together.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Without this team, no Valia, no Zandor, no cave fight, no ch.22 sacrifice. The entire B-plot (Marissa's reluctant love story) begins here.

### H-first-ley-line-opened — Marissa cracks open the first fount
- **Source chapter**: 5
- **Description**: Marissa uses her late-night, reinforced-channel spell to open a ley-line crossing in the mountain. Power erupts from the earth; the focusing tower begins to grow. The settlement now has a magic supply line. This is the first concrete demonstration of the magic-as-power-grid system that the trilogy's climax depends on.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Foundational magic-system establishment. The destruction of the receiver tower at the climax only works because this scene establishes how the towers feed the city.

### H-first-savage-encounter — Marissa fights two of the magic-eating creatures
- **Source chapter**: 6
- **Description**: Bathing in the stream, Marissa is attacked by two grotesque, violet-eyed, magic-devouring humanoids. Her spells are eaten, her ribs are broken, her hand is shattered. She kills one with a magic-flung dagger that exits the absorption aura before reaching its target.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Introduces the savages — the foe whose existence ultimately leads to the Groundlands' destruction. The aura mechanic is critical to the cave fight and the final horde assault.

### H-mother-shade-first-appears — Kyra manifests in the flesh-of-magic for the first time
- **Source chapter**: 6
- **Description**: When a third savage hurls a spear through Marissa's hip and is about to finish her, Kyra Zorantis appears as a glowing, transparent Archon-shaped figure with mismatched colours, kills the savage with the very same revolver Marissa carries, and cauterises Marissa's wound before fading. The Traitor is on-page, alive in some form, and watching over her daughter.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The single biggest reveal of book 1. The shade-of-Kyra is the spine of Marissa's interior life and the source of moral counterpoint for the rest of the trilogy.

### S-team-finds-savage-tracks — Felaan reports humanoid bare footprints
- **Source chapter**: 5–7
- **Description**: Before Marissa is attacked, Felaan finds barefoot humanoid tracks. The team agrees the creatures are real. Sets up rationale for Micah's later "genocide" reflex.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Pacing/setup beat that could be folded into another scene.

### H-the-dagger-is-the-vessel — Marissa works out that Kyra is in the gem
- **Source chapter**: 14 (with reader-side dawning across 6–14)
- **Description**: Marissa realises Kyra is not an Archon but a liche, bound inside the gem on the dagger Marissa carries — manifesting only where the magical field is dense enough. The talismanic object the rewrite leans into; the mechanism that controls when the shade can speak.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: This reveal is the lock-and-key of the mother-as-counsellor mechanic; every subsequent shade scene depends on it.

### H-uncle-kenny-tries-to-take-her-home — Kendall confronts Marissa on the mountain
- **Source chapter**: 9
- **Description**: At the second ley-line crossing, Kendall (Marissa's adopted uncle and the Bizhjerai master Construction Mage) catches her alone, demands she come home, threatens to destroy the Skylanders himself to save her. They fight; he is winning. Then the shade of Kyra steps between them, takes Marissa's side, and Kendall yields — but leaves her a summoning-crystal hidden as a contingency.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Establishes the off-ramp Marissa refuses, plants the Bizhjerai-fleet contingency that will arrive at the climax, and gives the reader Kyra's explicit blessing for the genocide-to-come. Plot-spine and theme-spine simultaneously.

### S-teach-zandor-to-burn-things — Marissa starts teaching the team magic
- **Source chapter**: 5, 8, 12
- **Description**: Over several scenes Marissa teaches Zandor, then Valia, then Wylah, to draw their first spells. Zandor stays at "throw fire". Valia rapidly reveals an aptitude for Construction.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Pacing / relationship-building. Could be compressed.

### S-alissa-arrives-in-disguise — "Alana" reveals herself to Marissa
- **Source chapter**: 13
- **Description**: A blue-haired woman intercepts Marissa in the muddy main street; it is Alissa Skylander (the Turncloak), Kendall's husband-equivalent in the Bizhjerai inner circle, here in disguise to back Marissa from the shadows. She gives Marissa a tattoo-spell that disguises the Bizhjerai Rune.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Important but could be re-shaped — the rune-disguise function could land via Kendall's gift instead. The character's arrival can be moved or merged.

### H-meet-alissa-as-shadow-ally — Alissa establishes herself as Marissa's invisible second
- **Source chapter**: 13
- **Description**: Whether the cover is "Alana Kelvari" or a different alias, Alissa stays in the camp as Marissa's covert hand: kills the man who recognises Marissa (ch.18), provides the poison Marissa later uses on the water supply, sabotages Micah's kill-teams when the rewrite shifts beats. The shadow-ally role itself.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Alissa's covert presence is mechanically required for the murder Marissa is later forced to perform, and for Marissa's final guilt-arc. Even if the specific scene is reshaped, the role must exist.

### S-malicious-compliance-walls — Marissa builds the walls quickly to spite Micah
- **Source chapter**: 12
- **Description**: Micah overrules Marissa's wall-design plan; she builds the walls anyway, quickly, in a way that drains all other magic supply. Their power-of-no rapport is calibrated. Micah later acknowledges he played her.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Texture for Marissa-Micah dynamic. Replaceable.

### S-zandor-and-valia-and-marissa — the threesome forms
- **Source chapter**: 11–13
- **Description**: Marissa and Valia kiss; Zandor walks in; the three of them tip into a polyamorous configuration. Wylah and Felaan become a couple in parallel. The book's emotional core takes its shape.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Crucial for emotional weight but the specific composition (V-shape vs other) is the orchestrator's call.

### H-the-farmers-are-murdered — Marissa sees the Skylander Way for what it is
- **Source chapter**: 15–17
- **Description**: Marissa and Zandor are sent south to negotiate trade with local farmers; they refuse. Micah orders the farms appropriated and the farmers and a nearby village killed. Zandor refuses the order. Days later, Marissa and friends find one of the farms — farmer, wife, daughter, dead by Skylander hands. Marissa's last hesitation about her mission breaks.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The moral threshold scene. Without this, Marissa's final destruction of 10,000 Skylanders has no on-page justification.

### S-hunt-down-the-raiders — gesture of goodwill toward the farms
- **Source chapter**: 15–16
- **Description**: Marissa, Zandor, Valia, Wylah, and Felaan ambush and wipe out a band of bandits to prove goodwill to the farmers. Operationally smooth. Earns the team confidence and tests them in combat.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Bonding action piece; replaceable with another shared-violence beat.

### S-the-recogniser-tries-to-kill-her — a Skylander recognises Marissa, Alissa kills him
- **Source chapter**: 18
- **Description**: At dusk, Marissa is recognised by a Skylander who tries to shoot her on sight; Alissa appears from the shadows and slits his throat. First on-page proof that Marissa's identity is fragile and that Alissa is working.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Atmosphere + foreshadowing. Can be folded into Alissa's first reveal or kept on its own.

### H-varl-arrives-and-blows-marissas-cover — the Prowler returns with the master she invented
- **Source chapter**: 19
- **Description**: A Skyship, the Prowler — a lost Skylander vessel that has been hunting other survivors for ten years — descends. From it disembarks Mathias Varl, the man Marissa claimed as her dead master. Varl declares he has never seen her in his life. Marissa is arrested. Her cover is destroyed.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The catastrophic mid-act reversal. Every subsequent suffering scene depends on this.

### H-the-trial-and-the-truth-extraction — Marissa, Varl's lie-detector, and a forced confession of half-truths
- **Source chapter**: 20
- **Description**: After days in a tiny dark box that drains her magic, Marissa is interrogated on stage. Varl casts a lie-detection spell. Marissa survives by speaking technically-true things misleadingly: she was raised by the Bizhjerai after her parents died, she came to join her people, she is a Construction Mage. Alissa, also captured, has signalled her to play a part.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The lie-detector reveal + Marissa's truth-but-not-the-whole-truth performance is the ethical and procedural setup for the murder she is about to be made to commit.

### H-marissa-shoots-alissa — the forced execution
- **Source chapter**: 20
- **Description**: Micah hands Marissa her own revolver loaded with one shot and orders her to kill Alissa, the woman Alissa claims raised her, to prove loyalty. Alissa signals her acceptance. Marissa shoots her between the eyes. The act severs Marissa morally from any remaining doubt.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The single most consequential character act in the trilogy. Defines Marissa's final arc; cannot be cut, off-screened, or replaced.

### H-marissa-survives-the-box — broken nose, days of dark, returned to her lovers
- **Source chapter**: 20–21
- **Description**: After the shooting, Marissa is thrown back in the box for days. She breaks her nose against the wall, scratches her fingernails off, screams herself raw, finally is released and carried home by Zandor and Valia, washed, fed, put to bed between them.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The pivot from active scheming to plan-with-cost. The lover-tenderness scene is the only thing standing between Marissa and complete moral collapse and is the seed of her final guilt.

### S-meta-magic-spell-conception — Valia's idea about spells designing spells
- **Source chapter**: 17
- **Description**: At the dam, Valia asks "what if a human didn't create the spell — what if a spell created it?" Marissa is stunned and begins prototyping a meta-magic search spell.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Beautiful character moment for Valia (the apprentice surpassing the teacher in a sideways way) and source of the in-world justification for Marissa's later miracles, but the specific concept can be reshaped or expanded into a beat per book.

### H-the-savage-trap-and-felaan-dies — the failed defence of the focusing tower
- **Source chapter**: 21–22
- **Description**: A red flare brings the team to the original focusing tower; it is a trap. Hundreds of savages drive them into caves. The team discovers the egg chamber — hundreds of dormant proto-savages stored in lovingly carved wooden walkways. The team is split. Felaan dies covering Wylah.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The savages are revealed not as a small tribe but as a numerous, organised, exponentially-hatching species; the protagonist's first irreplaceable loss; the catalyst for Micah's later war-footing decisions.

### H-valia-falls — Valia sacrifices herself off the cliff
- **Source chapter**: 22
- **Description**: Marissa and Valia, cornered on a high overlook, exchange a last kiss. Valia reveals she has known Marissa's true identity for months and loves her anyway. She pushes them both off the cliff, gifts Marissa all her magical power, then breaks out of Marissa's grip and falls to her death so that one of them survives.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy's central romantic-tragic beat. The act that converts Marissa from "I might still walk away" to "the Skylanders die." Cannot be cut, off-screened, or summarised.

### H-egg-chamber-as-foreshadow — the savages are children of an ancient spell
- **Source chapter**: 22, 28
- **Description**: The wooden ramp lined with nested eggs and the eventual reveal (via Kendall's tome in the epilogue) that the savages are pre-Apocalyptic experiments — humans engineered to live on magic alone, hunted to extinction, of whom a few eggs survived. The species is older than the Skylands themselves.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Sets up book 3 (or the trilogy's outer frame): the Wild Magic in the ruined Groundlands and the savages who will grow there. The trilogy's hook into a future Marissa-led era.

### H-marissa-poisons-the-water — using Alissa's hidden gift
- **Source chapter**: 24
- **Description**: Marissa finds the hidden compartment behind her workshop wall — the poison Alissa hinted at in her last words at the trial. She murders her covert tail (a young rose-haired woman) at the wall, hangs the body in deliberate Alissa-shadow style as misdirection, then plumbs the poison jug into the camp's water main. Days later the Groundlands is bedridden.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Concrete enactment of the betrayal plan. The poison is also one of the few causal threads that explains why the Groundlands cannot mount a full defence at the final battle.

### S-marissa-rigs-the-ammunition-magazines — the explosive power cores
- **Source chapter**: 23–24
- **Description**: While the savage horde marches on the wall, Marissa exploits the distraction to plant magic-explosive power cores in the central munitions caches and in the cliff redoubt magazines, all linked to a single trigger crystal.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Effective and clever but operationally reshapeable — the sabotage could be condensed or distributed.

### H-the-savage-horde-attacks — the Groundlands defends successfully (but bleeds)
- **Source chapter**: 24
- **Description**: An ordered horde of tens of thousands of savages — bred up from the egg chamber on the rich magic supply Marissa opened — assault the northern wall. The Skylander artillery, Angel air-mortar drops, and the Prowler's overwatch crush them. Thirty thousand savages die; the wall holds. Marissa, on the wall with Zandor, fights alongside them — the irony she is incapable of voicing — and uses the distraction to plant her sabotage.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The book's largest set piece and a critical inversion: the people Marissa is about to murder are also, in this moment, the only thing standing between her and the savages.

### H-the-bizhjerai-fleet-arrives — Kendall, Lukas, Raistlin's captain converge
- **Source chapter**: 25
- **Description**: Days after the savage attack, the fleet Marissa summoned with the hidden gem appears in the night sky and opens fire on the Groundlands shield. The Prowler launches to meet them. The endgame begins.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy's military climax requires the fleet to be in the sky; the arrival is the moment Marissa's plan becomes irreversible.

### H-zandor-knows-and-stands-in-her-way — the lovers' confrontation at the tower door
- **Source chapter**: 25
- **Description**: At the shield-tower door — guards killed by Zandor's intervention saving her from a shot — Zandor reveals he has known Marissa's identity since Valia knew, has loved her anyway, and will not let her destroy the Groundlands. They argue. Marissa shoots him through the thigh, cauterises the wound from inside the muscle with magic, and walks past him into the tower.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The hardest moral beat in the book — the man she loves, conscious and choosing, blocks her, and she chooses the mission anyway. The wound is non-fatal on purpose, but the rupture is total.

### H-marissa-destroys-the-shield-tower — the central sabotage
- **Source chapter**: 25–26
- **Description**: Inside the receiver tower Marissa adjusts the ratio between expansion and charging of the buried power cores so that they will rupture catastrophically. She triggers the magazine bombs. She seals the tower from outside as she leaves. The blast levels the camp's centre; the dome shield falls; the anti-air gunnery is half-blind.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The plan executes here. The trilogy's climax is the tower going up.

### H-marissa-kills-micah — the man who taught her
- **Source chapter**: 25
- **Description**: As Marissa walks away from the tower, Micah and his guards intercept her. He charges with a sword. She fans the hammer and shoots him in the heart with her mother's revolver, then drops the rest of the guards, then kills the Construction Mage chaperone with her mother's enchanted sword.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The death of Micah is the destruction-of-the-Skylander-future in person. Cannot be off-screened.

### H-the-prowler-falls — Kendall's bombardment cannon ends the last Skyship

- **Description**: On Kendall's flagship (the Red Terror), Marissa fights through boarding parties with the shade of Kyra at her side; Kendall takes the bridge, turns the ship prow-on to the Prowler, and uses the spinal bombardment cannon at a Skyship target — a shot no one should be able to make. ### H-genocide — the methodical sweep through the Groundlands

### H-im-the-last-skylander — the realisation

## Open notes for classification

- The mother-shade scenes (ch.6, 9, 10, 14, 19, 21, 25, 26, 27) collectively are essential. Individually most are reshapeable. The orchestrator should pick 3–4 of them to be hard (first manifestation, the dagger-reveal, the absolution-after-Alissa, the farewell) and treat the rest as soft.
- Several romantic beats (the threesome formation, the cliff-edge kiss, post-trial bedside care) deserve hard tier even though they are "character work" in genre terms — the trilogy's emotional spine collapses without them.
- The two acts of forced/refused intimate violence — Marissa shooting Alissa and Marissa shooting Zandor — both must remain hard. They are the moral cost the book pays.
- The pacing chapter "Construction Mage" (ch.12) contains soft beats (the teaching of Valia, the workshop, malicious-compliance walls) that the rewrite can distribute across the trilogy. None of the individual beats inside it is hard, but their accumulated effect — Marissa entrenching as indispensable while Valia rises beside her — is.
