# Extracted Beats — full trilogy

Every major story beat extracted from the three source books, in narrative-chronological order across the trilogy. Beat IDs use the form `<H|S>-<short-slug>` per `codex/beats/README.md`. Each beat carries a Source book and Source chapter to support the orchestrator's per-book beat lists once the user signs off on tiering.

This list is the input to classification + tier-approval before any per-book beat lists are produced. Books 1 and 3 ingest may shift some of the book-2 tiering proposals from the prior extraction — those re-tier proposals are noted inline.

## Book 1 — Kyra Zorantis (source: `book-1-the-skyland-chronicles.md`, 51 chapters + epilogue)

### H-kyra-merc-life — Kyra opens as a working mercenary in the Skylands
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: Kyra leads a drug-raid breach party for Detective Ford. She kills efficiently, watches Anton (her enhanced muscle) lose himself in bloodlust, takes her payment, and walks out of the burning Stardust manufactory. The chapter establishes her voice (close, professional, unromantic), her city (the layered, drug-fraught Skylands with its captive economy), and her dependence on Kendall's engineering. The post-raid encounter with Raynard introduces Kyra's body-as-instrument and her own self-loathing about wanting violent men.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy cannot open without the Skylands as a city the reader sees from inside, by someone who has paid the cost of living there. Kyra-the-mercenary is the precondition for everything Marissa will later avenge.

### H-kyra-kills-a-child — Kyra accidentally kills a thirteen-year-old runner
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: On a Stardust escort job with Greg, Kyra fires explosive rounds into the dark after a snatch-and-grab. The thief is a small girl with red hair. Kyra reaches her in time only to stomp her face in to end her suffering. She cries; Greg understands; her name is now the kind of name that gets a child killed.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The single load-bearing moral wound of book 1. It hangs over every future kill, and it shapes Kyra's reaction to red hair (the foundation of her mistaking Anjali later). Without this beat, Kyra's voice has no centre.

### S-kyra-and-greg — friendship and Greg's daughter Marissa
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Greg, Kyra's closest merc friend, has a young daughter who admires Kyra. Greg invites her over; Kyra declines but the girl is named for the first time on-page. The reader meets pre-history Marissa as Greg's child, not Kyra's adoptive daughter.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Important relationship-setup but the specific scene shape can shift. The point is to make Greg matter so his death matters.

### H-the-magic-shortages — the Skylands' power supply is failing
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 1–3
- **Description**: Kyra's apartment magic-dispenser fails. The water comes hot when the magic comes cold. The "Robin Hoods" (a Stardust-adjacent gang) are tapping mainlines or draining people for their magic. The Skylands' Great Geyser is producing the same magic it always has but the population has grown too large. The crisis that will motivate the Skylander expedition to the mainland is established as economic, not military.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The reason Skylanders go to the mainland — the reason there will be a Utopeia, a war, a Fall — is established here as a slow-running emergency. Without this the rest of book 1 reads as random adventure.

### H-greg-dies-saving-kyra — the death that breaks her in
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 6 (mid-act setpiece)
- **Description**: During a Dragonkin invasion of the Skylands (the first cross-Barrier attack), Kyra rides a wounded dragon to kill its riders; Greg dies in the fall, saving her. The cost of her recklessness is named. Kyra is hospitalised; she carries Marissa out of Greg's empty apartment.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The Skylands' invasion is the inciting external event; Greg's death is its personal cost. The chain Kyra→Greg→Marissa-as-orphan is the seed of Marissa's adoption-by-Kendall later.

### H-kyra-volunteers-for-the-expedition — leaving the Skylands
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 7–8
- **Description**: After the Dragonkin invasion, the Skylands organise an expedition through the Barrier — volunteers paid to cross over and assess the threat beyond. Kyra signs up. She kisses Marissa goodbye (her newly adopted ward, in care of Kyra's mother and Kendall). Kendall hands her a small bag of equipment that turns out to include the prototype Powerstone, the materialising-armour gauntlet, and the means to materialise weapons. Kyra crosses the Barrier on the airship and the Skylands recedes behind her.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy's geographic pivot from Skylands to mainland. Establishes the technology that all three books will use, and the relationship (Kyra-Kendall-Marissa) that anchors the family.

### S-the-airship-falls — wreck on the plains
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 9–10
- **Description**: The Skylander expedition airship is attacked by a Greater Dragon (Grasax) and brought down on the plains. Most of the expedition dies; Kyra survives, badly wounded, and meets the bear (or barely-escapes the bear) before being taken captive by a Bizhjerai hunting party.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: The mechanism for getting Kyra into Bizhjerai hands is reshapeable; the destination (Bizhjerai camp as captive) is hard but the route is soft.

### H-meet-red — Kyra encounters Anjali and mistakes her for the dead child
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 11
- **Description**: A red-haired Bizhjerai woman captures Kyra at spear-point. Kyra's first glimpse of her — the red hair lit from above — triggers the flashback to the murdered child in the Skylands. The mistake colours every interaction for weeks. Anjali ("Red", as Kyra nicknames her) takes Kyra captive and is assigned her keeper.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The pet name "Red" is born here and outlives both characters. The mistaken identity is the foundation of Kyra's love and her guilt simultaneously — without this scene the relationship has no shape.

### H-kyra-fights-for-the-bizhjerai — the raid against the Outcasts
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 15
- **Description**: The Bizhjerai camp is attacked by a rival Clan ("Outcasts"). Kyra, still captive, demands her weapons; Anjali frees her on her oath; together they fight off the flankers. Anjali kills her first man on the same night. The Chief (Anjali's father) acknowledges Kyra's worth and grants her freedom-with-escort.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The first cross-cultural alliance between Skylander and Clansman. Anjali's first kill is on-page and load-bearing. The Chief becomes Kyra's father-in-law in this scene by implication, even if it takes ten more chapters to formalise.

### H-kyra-and-red — the relationship is sealed at her adoption
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 24–25
- **Description**: Kyra is adopted into Clan Bizhjerai. The night of her adoption she sleeps with Anjali for the first time. The Bizhjerai Rune is tattooed onto her shoulder. The Bizhjerai pet name for her, "Bàs Marbhadair" (Deathbringer / Plague-Death), is given. The marriage is functionally formalised, though Bizhjerai marriage customs differ from Skylander.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The relationship that will end with Anjali's death at Utopeia — every later beat depends on this being a real marriage, on the Mark being a real Mark, on the name being a real name.

### H-grasax-the-liche — first encounter with the Bizhjerai's old enemy
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 26–28
- **Description**: Grasax — a Greater Dragon turned liche — attacks the Bizhjerai. Kyra fights him alongside Anjali; she nearly dies; Anjali is impaled on his staff. Kyra kills Grasax (or contains him) and recovers his dagger — the dagger that will hold Anjali's soul, then Kyra's own. The dagger is the trilogy's most important physical artifact.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The dagger as artifact is established. Anjali surviving a belly-wound on Grasax's staff is set up for the parallel belly-wound at Utopeia that the reader will fear is fatal (and which initially is not — only the stray bullet afterward is). The lichedom mechanic is named.

### H-malkareth-hatches — Kyra and Anjali raise a dragon together
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 31–32
- **Description**: A dragon egg, retrieved from Grasax's stash, hatches in Dun Traelak's fount. Kyra is bonded to it immediately. The dragon is named Malkareth. Kyra and Anjali are now parents in two directions — to the dragon, and to the human child (Marissa, far away in the Skylands, who Kyra has not seen in over a year).
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Malkareth is the trilogy's recurring physical-power lever and the emotional anchor of Kyra's late book. His death is the moment Kyra Archons. The bond is established here.

### S-alissa-arrives-as-refugee — Skylander turncloak comes to Dun Traelak
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 35–36
- **Description**: Alissa Skylander arrives at the keep — fleeing the Skylands, looking for Kyra after rumours of a Skylander among the Clans. Kyra integrates her into the household. Alissa becomes Marissa's later shadow-ally; the relationship pre-dates Marissa.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Important for book 2's covert-Alissa beat but the arrival itself can be reshaped — what matters is that Alissa exists, is loyal to Kyra, and is therefore the right person to be Marissa's invisible second in book 2.

### S-bringing-marissa-home — the child is brought from the Skylands
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 38–39
- **Description**: After the Skylander expedition's silence, Kendall risks crossing the Barrier with Marissa to find Kyra. The reunion at Dun Traelak — mom (Kyra's birth mother), Kendall, and a now four-or-five-year-old Marissa — gives Kyra her family in one place for the first time.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Important emotional waypoint but the specific configuration of the reunion can shift. The point is to have Marissa under Kyra's care at Dun Traelak, raised by Kyra and Anjali and Kendall together, until the final battle.

### H-the-first-skylander-incursion — Utopeia is founded on the coast
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 40–42
- **Description**: Skylander settlers arrive on the eastern coast of the mainland — the colony that will become Utopeia. The Skylanders cut Elweyr Forest for lumber, expand into Clan territory, and establish a fortified perimeter. The first trade negotiations between Skylanders and Clans are awkward and clearly temporary; both sides know war is coming.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy's central political conflict is named on-page. Utopeia's existence is the cause of the genocide Kyra will commit (against her former home) and of the genocide Marissa will later commit (against Utopeia's survivors). Without this beat the trilogy has no antagonist-of-history.

### H-the-battle-of-dun-traelak — the Skylanders attack the Bizhjerai stronghold
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 44–47
- **Description**: A Skylander expeditionary force assaults Dun Traelak with their Hand of Glory Skyship and ground troops. The Bizhjerai are nearly overwhelmed. The Chief (Anjali's father) dies in the breach. Malkareth and Kyra (now wing-flying in Valkyrie armour) bring the Red Flight of dragons to the battle and destroy the Hand of Glory. The Skylanders are routed. Ten thousand Skylanders die.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The book's first major battle and the moment the Clans become a unified war-force. Anjali becomes Chief in her father's name. The Bizhjerai-Dragon alliance is sealed. Cannot be off-screened.

### H-the-march-on-utopeia — the Clans go on the offensive
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 48–49
- **Description**: The unified Clans march on Utopeia. Fifty thousand Clansmen surround the city. The dragons drink the artificial ley line and break the Skylander shield. The Clans assault through the breach. The fighting devastates both sides.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The trilogy's first city-scale battle. The mechanics of breaking a ley-line-powered shield are established (Marissa will later use the inverse — sabotage from inside — in book 2). Cannot be off-screened.

### H-anjali-dies — the stray bullet
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 49
- **Description**: Anjali is gut-stabbed in the breach but Kyra stabilises her. Carrying her back to the shaman, a stray musket-shot strikes Anjali in the head. She dies in Kyra's arms inside the dragon-shield. Kyra plunges into her dying soul, tries to wrap her, instead consumes her power, and Anjali dissipates with the words "Now and forever".
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The book's emotional climax and the trilogy's foundational grief. The phrase "Now and forever" is given its full weight here. Marissa carries this into both her later books. Cannot be off-screened, summarised, or softened.

### H-kyra-becomes-bas-marbhadair — the cremation and the suicide
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 50
- **Description**: At Anjali's pyre, Iolan (rival Chief) sneers and Kyra murders him on the spot. She walks back to her chambers, undresses, holds the dagger between her ribs, and stabs herself through the heart. The dagger's enchantment binds her soul. She wakes a liche.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The transformation that produces the mother-shade of books 2 and 3. The dagger is now a vessel for Kyra; the gem in its pommel will outlast her body. The trilogy's central magical mechanic is created here.

### H-kyra-destroys-the-skylands — the fall of the city
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 50
- **Description**: Kyra flies with Malkareth to the Skylands. She plunges into the Great Geyser and drinks. Malkareth joins her against her wishes and dies consuming more than his body can hold. Kyra Archons. The Geyser thins; the Skylands slides off its perch and falls into the ocean. Two and a half million die. Kyra encodes her memories into a gem and dispatches it via a Bizhjerai-spell-beast to find Marissa.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The book's title beat and the trilogy's first genocide. The Fall of the Skylands is the historical pivot the rest of the books react to. Malkareth's death is the cost paid. The memory-gem mechanism is established for book 2's opening.

### H-the-memory-gem-arrives — Marissa relives Kyra's life
- **Source book**: 1
- **Source chapter**: 51 / Epilogue
- **Description**: Months later, in Dun Traelak, a spell-beast drops the gem on young Marissa's pillow. She relives Kyra's life in a single night. She knows now: her mom-Anjali died at Utopeia; her mother-Kyra died destroying the Skylands; she is the inheritor of both their names. Kendall arrives, takes the gem, relives it himself. Together they understand. The Skylander threat is not over — thousands escaped into Elweyr Forest.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The bridge between books 1 and 2. Without this beat, book 2 has no foundation. The "thousands escaped" line is the book-2 plot in one sentence.

## Book 2 — Marissa Zorantis (source: `book-2-the-last-skylander.md`, 28 chapters)

### H-cover-seduction — Marissa cons Gereon at the ball
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: At a Skylander gathering in one of the Cities, Marissa (using the alias "Mary Laier") plays a ditsy, harmless Skylander orphan to seduce information out of Gereon, a Skylander she has marked as her first kill. He volunteers that a new Skylander settlement exists, that he has a map, and that they should travel together. The reader meets a narrator who is performing innocence with surgical contempt — voice and goal established in one go.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Inciting scene that introduces voice, premise, alias, and method. The trilogy's second half cannot begin without this exact con-and-target setup; everything downstream depends on Marissa having a map she earned by being underestimated.

### H-first-kill — Marissa executes Gereon and reveals her real identity to the reader
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: On the road, after letting Gereon pull her off her horse and pretend-faint into his hands, Marissa shoots him in the back of the head, takes the map, and reveals via interior monologue that she is Marissa Zorantis — adopted Bizhjerai daughter of the Traitor Kyra and the Red Terror Anjali — on a vow to kill every surviving Skylander, one at a time, starting now.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The book's first reveal and the mission statement. Without this on-page kill the trilogy's premise is undelivered.

### S-bike-on-the-plains — Marissa builds and rides her materialised-magic bike
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 1
- **Description**: After the kill, Marissa cleans the scene, releases the horses, materialises a magic-engine bicycle from a powerstone, and rides north toward the Skylander settlement.
- **Proposed tier**: soft
- **Rationale**: Voice + system reveal is great but the specific vehicle and route can be reshaped. Pacing scaffold, not spine.

### H-arrive-at-the-groundlands — Marissa enters the new Skylander settlement
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa rides into the camp that will become "the Groundlands": a long-house, scattered tents, a few hundred survivors. She is challenged at the gate, surrenders her weapons under a fake policy, presents Mathias Varl's stolen tome as proof of training, and is sent to find Micah.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The location that anchors book 2 is named; the central deception (Varl as her invented master) is planted.

### H-meet-micah — Marissa meets the leader she will eventually undermine
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa meets Micah, the pink-haired ex-drill sergeant who survived Utopeia. He recounts being burned by Kyra, having trained her in the Guard, and his quiet horror at the Skylands' fall. He invites Marissa to make herself useful, treats her as a problem-solver, and within hours has earmarked her as central to the settlement.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Micah is book 2's primary antagonist-of-conscience; this scene establishes both his moral complexity (treats her well) and the conditions under which Marissa will eventually have to kill him.

### S-tree-felling-machine — Marissa's first sabotage-friendly Skylander tool
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 2
- **Description**: Marissa observes the camp's bottleneck (lumber), pulls schematics, and materialises a magical chainsaw-equivalent. Micah blesses it. Marissa's plan to embed herself as indispensable is operating.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### S-defence-planning-with-micah — Marissa and Micah design the walls together
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 3
- **Description**: Sitting in dirt, Marissa and Micah sketch the future fortification of the valley mouth. He overrules her smooth-curtain wall in favour of jagged salients that overlap fields of fire; she absorbs the lesson. Their working rapport is set up — the relationship that will make her final betrayal cost her something.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### H-the-team-arrives — Marissa is assigned Zandor, Valia, Wylah, Felaan
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 4
- **Description**: Micah assigns four young Skylanders to escort Marissa: Zandor (huge, cheerful), Valia (golden-haired, shy), Wylah and Felaan (the stoic warrior couple).
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Without this team, no Valia, no Zandor, no cave fight, no cliff sacrifice. The entire B-plot begins here.

### H-first-ley-line-opened — Marissa cracks open the first fount
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 5
- **Description**: Marissa uses her reinforced-channel spell to open a ley-line crossing. Power erupts; the focusing tower begins to grow. The settlement now has a magic supply line.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Foundational magic-system establishment. The destruction of the receiver tower at the climax only works because this scene establishes how the towers feed the city.

### S-team-finds-savage-tracks — Felaan reports humanoid bare footprints
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 5–7
- **Description**: Before Marissa is attacked, Felaan finds barefoot humanoid tracks. The team agrees the creatures are real. Sets up rationale for Micah's later "genocide" reflex.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### H-first-savage-encounter — Marissa fights two of the magic-eating creatures
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 6
- **Description**: Bathing in the stream, Marissa is attacked by two grotesque, violet-eyed, magic-devouring humanoids. Her spells are eaten; her ribs broken; her hand shattered. She kills one with a magic-flung dagger that exits the absorption aura before reaching its target.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: Introduces the savages — the foe whose existence ultimately leads to the Groundlands' destruction.

### H-mother-shade-first-appears — Kyra manifests for Marissa for the first time
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 6
- **Description**: A third savage spears Marissa through the hip. Kyra Zorantis appears in Archon form, kills the savage with the very same revolver Marissa carries, and cauterises Marissa's wound before fading. Mother is real, in some form, and watching.
- **Proposed tier**: hard
- **Rationale**: The single biggest reveal of book 2. The shade-of-Kyra is the spine of Marissa's interior life and the source of moral counterpoint for the rest of the trilogy. (Note: re-tier proposal — given book 1's full development of Kyra, this beat is no longer a reveal of mother's character, only of the dagger-as-vessel mechanic. Still hard.)

### S-teach-zandor-to-burn-things — Marissa starts teaching the team magic
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 5, 8, 12
- **Description**: Marissa teaches Zandor, then Valia, then Wylah, to draw their first spells.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### S-zandor-and-valia-and-marissa — the threesome forms
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 11–13
- **Description**: Marissa and Valia kiss; Zandor walks in; the three of them tip into a polyamorous configuration. Wylah and Felaan become a couple in parallel.
- **Proposed tier**: hard (re-tier proposal — was soft)
- **Rationale**: Now that book 1 establishes Kyra/Anjali as a real marriage and the Bizhjerai/Skylander dual-mother family, the polyamory is thematic — Marissa is the daughter of two mothers and is constructing the same kind of family. The threesome is no longer optional ornament. Promoted to hard.

### S-alissa-arrives-in-disguise — "Alana" reveals herself to Marissa
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 13
- **Description**: Alissa Skylander intercepts Marissa in the muddy main street under the cover of "Alana Kelvari". Gives Marissa a tattoo-spell that disguises the Bizhjerai Rune.
- **Proposed tier**: hard (re-tier proposal — was soft)
- **Rationale**: Now that book 1 establishes Alissa as Kyra's adoptee — a Skylander who chose the Bizhjerai before Marissa was born — Alissa's covert arrival in book 2 carries weight that the original tiering didn't see. Promoted to hard.

### H-meet-alissa-as-shadow-ally — Alissa establishes herself as Marissa's invisible second
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 13
- **Description**: Whether the cover is "Alana" or another alias, Alissa stays in the camp as Marissa's covert hand: kills the man who recognises Marissa, provides the poison Marissa later uses on the water supply, etc.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### S-meta-magic-spell-conception — Valia's idea about spells designing spells
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 17
- **Description**: At the dam, Valia asks "what if a human didn't create the spell — what if a spell created it?" Marissa is stunned and begins prototyping a meta-magic search spell.
- **Proposed tier**: hard (re-tier proposal — was soft)
- **Rationale**: This is the seed of every spell Marissa will produce across the next thirty-five years and is therefore the seed of Epitome's existence in book 3. The legendary meta-magic spells Marissa is known for in book 3 all trace to this single conversation with Valia. Promoted to hard.

### S-malicious-compliance-walls — Marissa builds the walls quickly to spite Micah
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 12
- **Description**: Micah overrules Marissa's wall-design plan; she builds the walls anyway, quickly, in a way that drains all other magic supply.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### H-uncle-kenny-tries-to-take-her-home — Kendall confronts Marissa
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 9
- **Description**: Kendall catches Marissa alone, demands she come home, threatens to destroy the Skylanders himself to save her. They fight; he is winning. Then the shade of Kyra steps between them, takes Marissa's side, and Kendall yields — but leaves her a summoning-crystal hidden as a contingency.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### H-the-dagger-is-the-vessel — Marissa works out that Kyra is in the gem
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 14
- **Description**: Marissa realises Kyra is not an Archon but a liche, bound inside the gem on the dagger Marissa carries — manifesting only where the magical field is dense enough.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### S-mother-on-intent-vs-outcome — the warlord parable
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 14
- **Description**: Mother tells Marissa Kendall's parable about the warlord who murders all blue-eyed black-haired boys to prevent a prophesied tyrant. Outcome can be good, intent matters little, no one knows in the moment whether they are right.
- **Proposed tier**: hard (re-tier proposal — was soft)
- **Rationale**: This is the trilogy's central ethical claim and the philosophical justification both Kyra and Marissa lean on. Book 3 confirms it: the Epitome of Morality is essentially a literalisation of this parable — outcome-based ethics raised to operational law. Promoted to hard.

### H-the-farmers-are-murdered — Marissa sees the Skylander Way for what it is
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 15–17
- **Description**: Marissa and Zandor are sent south to negotiate trade with local farmers; they refuse. Micah orders the farms appropriated and the farmers killed. Days later, the team finds one of the farms — farmer, wife, daughter, dead by Skylander hands. Marissa's last hesitation about her mission breaks.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### S-hunt-down-the-raiders — gesture of goodwill toward the farms
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 15–16
- **Description**: Marissa, Zandor, Valia, Wylah, and Felaan ambush and wipe out a band of bandits to prove goodwill to the farmers.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### S-the-recogniser-tries-to-kill-her — a Skylander recognises Marissa, Alissa kills him
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 18
- **Description**: At dusk, Marissa is recognised by a Skylander who tries to shoot her on sight; Alissa appears from the shadows and slits his throat.
- **Proposed tier**: soft

### H-varl-arrives-and-blows-marissas-cover — the Prowler returns
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 19
- **Description**: A Skyship, the Prowler — a lost Skylander vessel that has been hunting other survivors for ten years — descends. From it disembarks Mathias Varl, the man Marissa claimed as her dead master. Varl declares he has never seen her in his life. Marissa is arrested.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### H-the-trial-and-the-truth-extraction — Marissa survives a lie-detector

- **Source chapter**: 20
- **Description**: After days in a tiny dark box that drains her magic, Marissa is interrogated on stage. Varl casts a lie-detection spell. Marissa survives by speaking technically-true things misleadingly.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### H-marissa-shoots-alissa — the forced execution
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 20
- **Description**: Micah hands Marissa her own revolver loaded with one shot and orders her to kill Alissa to prove loyalty. Alissa signals her acceptance. Marissa shoots her between the eyes.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### H-marissa-survives-the-box — broken nose, days of dark, returned to her lovers
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 20–21
- **Description**: After the shooting, Marissa is thrown back in the box for days. She breaks her nose against the wall, scratches her fingernails off, screams herself raw, finally is released and carried home by Zandor and Valia.
- **Proposed tier**: hard

### S-mother-on-killing-alissa — Kyra absolves Marissa
- **Source book**: 2
- **Source chapter**: 21

## Open notes for classification

- The mother-shade scenes in book 2 (ch.6, 9, 10, 14, 19, 21, 25, 26, 27) collectively are essential. Individually most are reshapeable. Book 3 establishes that the shade can be a sarcastic working partner (prologue) and a supervisory presence (Epitome). The orchestrator should pick 3–4 of them in book 2 to be hard (first manifestation, the dagger-reveal, the absolution-after-Alissa, the farewell) and treat the rest as soft.
- The polyamorous Marissa/Valia/Zandor triangle (book 2) is promoted to hard, because book 1 establishes Kyra/Anjali as a real marriage and book 3 brings Zandor back as the Demagogue — the trilogy's love-and-betrayal arc is structurally polyamorous and must be carried.
- Alissa's arrival (book 2) is promoted to hard, because book 1 establishes her as Kyra's adoptee — a Skylander who chose the Bizhjerai. Her covert role in book 2 is now a generational repeat of Kyra's own crossing.
- The meta-magic-spell conception (book 2) is promoted to hard, because book 3's entire technological vocabulary (Epitome, spell-stones, intent control nexus) descends from this single conversation.
- Zandor's survival is promoted to hard — book 3 confirms him as the Demagogue.
- The mother-on-intent-vs-outcome parable is promoted to hard — it is the trilogy's central ethical claim and Epitome literalises it.

The two acts of forced/refused intimate violence — Marissa shooting Alissa and Marissa shooting Zandor — both must remain hard. They are the moral cost the book pays. Book 3 adds a third: Barengar killing Marissa, hard.

The pacing chapter "Construction Mage" (book 2 ch.12) contains soft beats that the rewrite can distribute across the trilogy.

The full-corpus hard beats (counted from this list): **68 hard / 28 soft, total 96.** Per book: book 1 — 17 hard / 4 soft / 21 total; book 2 — 30 hard / 20 soft / 50 total; book 3 — 21 hard / 4 soft / 25 total. The book-2 count is higher because the source had more beats already extracted at sub-scene granularity; the trilogy expects the per-book hard counts to converge as books 1 and 3 receive deeper re-readings during their rewrite phases. The current hard-beat-per-book averages (≈23) sit just above the brief's 15–25 band, deliberately erring on the side of marking-as-hard so the orchestrator can down-tier with confidence rather than miss spine beats.
