# Book 4 — Infiltrator

## Spine

Marissa Zorantis — twenty-two, trained, in alias as "Mary Laier" — walks into a Skylander-survivor ball, cocks her hip at Gereon, charms a map out of him, rides out of the city with him, and shoots him in the back of the head on the road. She rides north to the Skylander settlement she has been hunting for half a decade and walks in at the gate as a problem-solver. By book's end she has cracked open the first artificial ley line for the camp's Mages, been assigned a team of four Skylanders her own age, fought a magic-eating savage and survived because a shade in the shape of her dead mother shot the third one for her, and refused her uncle Kendall's offer to take her home. The infiltration is working. Her family knows where she is. The shade is watching. Her mother is real, in some form, in a gem on her hip.

## Opening image

A ballroom. Crystal chandeliers, materialised-magic dresses, Skylander-survivor wealth. A small woman in a dress that is the wrong shape for her — too much cleavage, too little spine — making her eyes wide for a tall man at the refreshments table. She holds his gaze a beat too long, drops her eyes, smiles. The reader does not yet know whether she is the prey or the hunter. The first line of dialogue is hers.

## Closing image

A cold mountain switchback. Kendall on his knees, blood on his temple, the summoning-crystal he has slipped into Marissa's pocket the only word he has not said aloud. Marissa standing over him with her revolver and an open palm. Between them, the shape of a woman in violet light. Marissa walking down the switchback alone, the crystal in her pocket warm against her hip. The shade walking beside her without footprints.

## Theme

What you owe the people who raised you when the people you intend to kill are also the people who raised them.

## Target word count

95–105k. Sits at the lower end of the band. The book has nine hard beats but most are scene-shaped, not arc-shaped — they each land in roughly one chapter. The book's job is to establish the voice, the alias, the camp, the team, the savages, the shade, and Kendall as the man Marissa can refuse but cannot avoid. The expansion budget goes to the team chemistry (which has to earn the polyamory in Book 5) and to the camp's day-to-day (which has to earn the betrayal in Book 6).

## What's not in this book (leapt)

- Fifteen years of young-Marissa-in-Dun-Traelak. Not staged. Referenced only when relevant to a present-tense judgement.
- The full polyamorous configuration with Zandor and Valia — held for Book 5. The team is chemistry-only in Book 4.
- Alissa's arrival in camp — held for Book 5. Alissa is referenced in interior monologue as the surrogate aunt Marissa was raised by; she does not appear on-page.
- The farmers — the southern run is teased only; the killings happen in Book 5.
- Valia's revelation of having known Marissa's identity. Held for Book 6.
- Zandor's revelation likewise. Held for Book 6.
- Most of the camp's politics. Micah is a working leader, not yet a man Marissa has to kill.
