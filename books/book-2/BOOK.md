# Book 2 — Partnership

## Spine

Kyra, captive of the Bizhjerai at book's open, becomes their dragon-rider and their war-chief's wife by book's close. She fights the Clan's old liche-enemy and wins a dagger she does not yet know is meant for her own ribs. She hatches a dragon and renames it for the trade-tongue of her wife. She is reunited with the daughter she left in the Skylands when Kendall walks her out of the floating city for the last time. By book's end the Skylanders have crossed the Barrier in force, built a stockade on the eastern coast, and named it Utopeia — and Kyra is standing on Dun Traelak's walls with a Clan she has come to belong to, looking east, knowing the war is now her war.

## Opening image

Kyra in a Bizhjerai pavilion in the dawn cold, the dagger she took from a dead man on her belt and a red-haired woman's hand on her shoulder. The smell of horse and tea. The Outcast raid is two days behind them; the next ride is two days ahead. The Chief has summoned her to the council fire and she does not yet know whether she is being asked for or asked to leave.

## Closing image

Kyra in Valkyrie armour on Dun Traelak's outer wall, the wings folded, Marissa asleep in her chamber a hundred yards behind her, Anjali asleep at her shoulder, Malkareth coiled in the inner court. To the east, on the horizon, smoke from the Skylander colony's hearth-fires. The first dragon-patrol of the morning rises off the keep. The voice closes on a sentence that has been the book's spine without ever being said aloud — that there is no version of this in which her daughter does not have to know what is coming.

## Theme

Adoption — by a Clan, by a wife, by a dragon, by the child she has decided to raise; and the cost of being adopted by something that is going to ask you to kill your old people.

## Voice

Kyra Zorantis, in her partnered register. See `codex/voice/voice-profile.md` §Kyra (cadence, recurring imagery, anchors). The voice softens around Anjali and Malkareth; hardens around the colonial threat. Distance from the action stays close; the editorial stays tactical.

## Target word count

100–105k. Sits at the middle of the band. The book is structurally a long second act, with three set pieces (Grasax, the hatching, the colony's arrival) and a domestic backbone. The source spends more time here than the rewrite should — the magic-system exposition (S-tier work in Books 4–6) starts here at lower density and the reader does not yet need the full theory.

## What's not in this book (leapt)

- The actual marriage ceremony in any externalised form — Bizhjerai customs differ; the bonding is functional, not ceremonial.
- Most of the Skylander reunion logistics (Kendall's defection, the airship crossing). The defection happens off-page; the reader meets Kendall and Marissa as having arrived, not as arriving.
- The full politics of the Clan alliance. Other Chiefs exist and are named; the unification that drives Book 3's war is named only as coming.
- Raynard's death — depending on orchestrator choice, may sit at the head of Book 2 as a hinge scene or be retained at Book 1's close. If retained at Book 1, it is referred to in this book only as past tense.

## Hooks into the next book

- The dagger — recovered from Grasax, gem-pommelled, now stored at Dun Traelak. The reader sees what lichedom is and the artifact that does it. The use of the artifact on Kyra's own chest is Book 3's pivot.
- The colony — named, fortified, trading. The reader knows war is coming. Book 3 opens after a season of raids and pivots to the winter siege.
- Marissa — present at Dun Traelak by book's close; the reader has met her as Kyra's daughter, not as Marissa-the-future-protagonist. The reunion scene at the marketplace is held for Book 3's opening.
- Kendall — defected, building. The bombardment cannon and the gauntlet engineering are seeded here. Most of his work happens between books.
- Anjali's gut-wound, survived. The reader knows wounds like this can be survived. The bullet that does not let her be survived is Book 3's central grief.
