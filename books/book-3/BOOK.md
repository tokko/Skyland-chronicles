# Book 3 — The Fall

## Spine

Kyra opens the book reunited with her daughter at a Clan marketplace and closes it as a liche, having drained the Skylands' Great Geyser and killed her own people, her own home, and her own dragon-brother in a single afternoon. Between those two images the Skylander colonists assault Dun Traelak, the Clans rally and march on Utopeia, and Anjali — survivor of every wound Kyra has watched her take — is killed by one stray bullet in the breach. Kyra burns her wife on a pyre, kills the rival Chief who sneers at the cremation, walks back to her own chamber and pushes Grasax's dagger through her heart. She wakes a liche. She flies one last time. She kills two and a half million people because she does not know how else to stop the next wave. The book ends not in her body but in her daughter's — a thirteen-year-old in Dun Traelak waking on a pillow where a spell-beast has just placed a memory-gem.

## Opening image

The Bizhjerai marketplace at Dun Traelak. Clansmen and Skylanders trading wool for materialised-magic gems. A child shrieks "Mom!" and runs at Kyra full tilt. Marissa folds into her arms as if she belongs there — and she does. Kendall and Kyra's mother are a few yards behind. Tears in everyone's eyes. The world shrinks to three. Half a year after Book 2's close. Marissa is now five. Kyra is glowing.

## Closing image

A thirteen-year-old girl sitting on her bed in Dun Traelak, the smell of summer through an open window, a fist-sized dodecahedron of crystal lying loose against her thigh. Her mother is in it. Her mother is dead. Her uncle is at the door, frightened. She has just lived through years of someone else's life in seconds and she has come out of it knowing one thing — that the Skylanders her mother killed were not all of them, and that she is going to finish what her mother started. The book closes on a child's vow that the next book will spend a hundred thousand words honouring.

## Theme

What love looks like when the people who taught you it are dead, and what it costs to inherit them.

## Target word count

95–105k. Sits at the lower end of the band. The book is short by design — three set pieces, one grief-aria, one cataclysm, one epilogue. The source's pacing here is already lean; the rewrite should resist padding. The grief must land; the cataclysm must land; nothing else.

## What's not in this book (leapt)

- The full Clan unification — other Chiefs sign on; the rally happens between chapters; the assembly is not staged at length. Kendall's bombardment-cannon engineering, the Bizhjerai fleet's construction, the Reaver alliances — all happen off-page and arrive as facts.
- The fifteen years of young Marissa in Dun Traelak under Kendall, Alissa, and Marie — held entirely for the gap between Books 3 and 4. The epilogue is the last we see of Marissa as a child; the next time she walks on-page she is twenty-two and in a Skylander ballroom.
- Most of the actual mechanics of Kyra's liche transformation — the source explains them at length; the rewrite shows the act and lets the magic file in `codex/magic/liche-enchantment.md` carry the theory.

## Hooks into the next book

- The memory-gem and the dagger — both now Marissa's. The artifact that opens Book 4 is in her belt by Book 4 ch.1; the reader knows what it contains.
- "Thousands escaped into Elweyr forest" — the line Marissa says to herself in the epilogue. This is Book 4's premise in advance.
- Kyra-in-the-gem — the reader knows there is a soul in the dagger. Marissa does not know how to wake it. Book 4 ch.6 makes her find out.
- The Bizhjerai Rune on the bones — Kyra wore it; Marissa will wear it; the magic file is established.
- Kendall surviving — alive, devastated, holding the gem. Marissa's "Uncle Kenny" for the next fifteen years and the man who tries to take her home in Book 4.
