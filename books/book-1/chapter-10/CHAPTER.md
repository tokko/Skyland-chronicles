# Book 1, Chapter 10 — The Chief's Judgement

## Source coverage
source-book-1 / ch.13 (the trial — the Chief's questions, the recounting of Savistlin, the crowd's disbelief, the shaman's silent presence, the verdict, the Chief naming "Anjali" for the first time, Red's frustration at being saddled with guard duty) and source-book-1 / ch.14 (compressed — the days of integration that follow the trial: meals with the camp, the wood-smoke meat, sparring with Red, the new vocabulary, the lazy week before the Outcast raid).

## POV
Kyra Zorantis (first person, retrospective past tense, close — see voice §Kyra)

## Spine
The trial. Kyra stands in front of the Chief — Red's father, who looks more like her than Kyra had expected — and answers questions about where she comes from and how she got here. The crowd disbelieves the dragon story. The shaman is in attendance, silent, in horns and bone. The Chief withholds judgement and consults the Elements. He decrees that Kyra will remain in the camp under Anjali's escort — and in the same breath he speaks Anjali's name in dialog for the first time, and Anjali bristles at being saddled with the duty. Days follow. Kyra eats wood-fire-grilled buffalo and discovers it is the best meal of her life. The camp adapts to her in slow steps. She spars with Anjali daily — sword-work where Kyra has the experience and unarmed-work where Anjali has the speed — and the friendship deepens through proximity. She does not see the Chief again that week. External goal: survive the trial without lying transparently, find a footing in the Bizhjerai camp, learn enough of their world to be useful. Internal goal: discover that being a guest is sometimes harder than being a prisoner, and that the camp's life is a thing she could want.

## Beats this chapter advances
- H-meet-red — the relationship moves from keeper-and-captive to companion-by-decree. Anjali's name lands in dialog for the first time in scene-01's closing.
- H-kyra-and-red — foreshadowed. The sparring scenes carry the relationship's physical undercurrent. The chapter ends with the friendship a fact and the romance still ahead.
- H-grasax-the-liche — named in passing during the trial dialog (the Chief mentions an "old enemy" the Clans fight, hooked but not explained — Grasax is named in chapter 11 after the raid).

## Beats this chapter LANDS
- _none._ This is the structural breather between the trial-as-hard-test (the second-to-last test before the Outcast raid) and the raid itself. The chapter's load-bearing function is the friendship's deepening; no hard beat lands.

## Opening hook
The pavilion. The Chief in his folding chair — ginger beard, ginger hair, the same set of features Kyra has been looking at across a fire for two days, only male and twenty years older. The pieces clicking. Anjali's wince of recognition.

## Closing hand-off
A clear evening at the end of the week. The smell of wood-smoke from the cooking fires. Kyra sharpening a wooden knife Anjali has given her for slow-work. A horn at the edge of the camp. Anjali on her feet without a word. The chapter ends on the alarm.

## Writer notes
- **The Chief is voiced as a sergeant-king.** Loud, simple, declarative. The source likens his voice to a drill sergeant's; the rewrite preserves the parade-ground register. His questions are short; his pauses do the rhetorical work. The "It sounds like Savistlin and her children" line is in his mouth, and the trilogy's later cosmology (dragon-Flights named by colour, dragons with personal names) is established in this single beat.
- **The shaman is voiceless in this chapter.** He stands silent at the Chief's side. The chapter establishes his existence and his magical-presence; his voice waits for Book 2.
- **Anjali's name** lands in the Chief's mouth at scene-01's close — "I believe you are right, Anjali, but she is still an outsider". The rewrite preserves this as the chapter's revelation. Kyra registers the name and continues to use "Red" privately.
- **The cuffs remain on through the chapter.** Kyra is not free; she is on long lead. The chapter does not let the reader forget this — every spar, every meal, every bath happens with the bronze at her wrists and ankles.
- **The week of integration is compressed.** Source-ch.14 covers it in a long looping sequence; the rewrite gives the week one scene (scene-03) and trusts the reader to take it on faith. The detail-density goes into the trial and the first wood-fire meal.
- **No "I lied" tags.** Kyra withholds some truths from the Chief (Alissa's existence, the city's true scale). The rewrite does not flag the withholdings to the reader; the reader notices on their own.
- **Pacing target.** Roughly 7–9k words.
- **The chapter ends mid-action.** Not on a tableau, not on a "Time to fight" closer. Just on the alarm-horn. The next chapter opens inside the raid.
