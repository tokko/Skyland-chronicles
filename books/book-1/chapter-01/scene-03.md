The fastest of the three reached for his ramrod, Anton's body on the floor between us. The walkway above ran in shadow between the sparse globes along its rail.

From up on the gantry, a body came down into the hall.

It hit the crystal floor and went still. I heard, before I saw, the volley that followed, three close-spaced reports from the walkway above, smoothbores, the mana-discharge sound less sharp than a rifle and spreading wider. A second breach party, come up another way, firing down into the hall. At this range a smoothbore was as likely to put a crystal slug through me as through the men they were shooting at. I dropped to one knee and raised my left arm.

The shield manifested from the gauntlet, translucent, humming at the frequency it always hummed when it had work to do. One round cracked against it and scattered into indigo fragments off to my left. Another of the men across the hall went down. The third shot hit the far wall somewhere past them. The Powerstone at my throat stepped down a notch. The man with his hand on his ramrod had not gotten it out yet. I dismissed the shield and came up with the primary revolver and shot him through the head. He dropped. The third man had his back to me before I cocked the hammer the second time. I shot him between the shoulder blades and he flopped forward onto his face, sliding a few inches on the crystal, and lay there kicking once.

Two rounds in the primary. Four in the backup.

I looked up at the gantry.

Five men up there. They were already working their ramrods, heads down, the smooth muscle-memory of a drill. Not from any contract I knew; they had come up a back route and cleared their own floor before arriving here. The one at the near rail was watching me instead of his reload. A good-looking man, dark-skinned, hair the colour of fungal gold that the glow globe lit to something brighter. He had a smudge of grime from his jaw to his left cheekbone that was going to be there through tomorrow. He brushed a loose curl back from his face and got a second smudge on his forehead for the trouble.

He raised his chin at me. Not a salute, exactly. An acknowledgement.

I lifted the longer sword off my hip and pointed the blade at him, flat side up, and let it come back down.

They were good. Seventeen seconds, and all five had their muskets primed.

I crossed the hall and went up the stairs.

"Follow me," I said when I reached them. "I'll tank. You shoot. Our target's at the end of that hallway, holed up or gone, one or the other. Fix your bayonets. It's about to get messy."

There was a short corridor beyond the gantry: four doors, all closed, two on each side. Sporadic firing from below, dimmed by the floors between. The blond man was behind my right shoulder and the other four spread across the width of the corridor after him. We were six, which was enough if the target had his doors shut and his men inside. Not enough if the target had called for reinforcements and gotten them.

At the far end, a set of double doors. Crystal, like everything else, but the carvings on them were the expensive kind. The manufactory's cartel boss did not economise on his own office.

Two more mercs came up the stair behind us before we reached the doors. Women, both of them. The one with the braids had red hair and a spear and a cut across her left cheek that was still seeping and had been for a while, the blood dried in a line down to her jaw and wet at the wound's edge. Her partner wore a vest covered in plates of crystal bonded to the padding in overlapping rows, crude armour, the kind you built from what was available rather than what was designed, but she had made it and the shoulder I could see had a bruise the size of my hand developing under the sleeve where the jacket had saved her from something that would otherwise have been through her. Two smoothbore pistols, one on each hip.

"There room?" the redhead said.

"Get behind me."

I expanded my magesense into the door.

The Powerstone at my throat was at a fifth, maybe a little above, reading thin. The gantry shield had taken another step down. Enough for a breach. Probably not for a sustained fight with a mage inside, but there was not always a mage inside, and there was one way to find out.

I blasted the lock apart and kicked the door open and stepped into the barrier as it manifested, leaning forward with my left arm extended and my weight behind the shield. The pane was larger than usual; I pushed the edges sideways to cover the mercs on either side. Someone in the room fired at the opening and the rounds cracked against the shield and went wide. I kept pushing forward.

What I saw stopped me moving for half a second.

They had dragged the women in from somewhere below, from a cooking floor, wherever they had found them. Twelve, maybe fifteen. Stripped to what the cartel stripped the workers to. They had them in a row across the far end of the room, not quite shoulder to shoulder, close enough that the firing line behind them would land somewhere on a woman no matter where you aimed if you put it at centre mass. The men behind that row had their firearms up. A handful more stood with melee weapons at their sides, waiting.

The women in the front row were holding knives and makeshift clubs, pipe-lengths, a crystal stool-leg. Most of them were crying. The one directly in front of me had pissed herself; the liquid was still going down her thighs.

My companions had the same read I had. Nobody fired. The men behind the human shield line had calibrated this exactly: they would shoot or cut down any woman who moved out of position, and they had, as far as they were concerned, a standoff.

Two of the women broke at once.

They ran in different directions, one left and one right, and the men behind them closed the gap. The one running left was cut down by a chopping sword. The one running right took a round through the shoulder from a smoothbore and went down against the wall and stayed there. Then the others who had been trying to hold still gave up all at once, and it went wrong everywhere simultaneously.

My companions fired into the chaos. I stepped through.

A woman at the end of the line howled and fell as one of the gantry mercs' rounds tore a breast apart at the wrong angle; the blood ran down her ribs before she hit the crystal. I did not stop moving. A panicking woman with tears all over her face and a knife raised came at me directly and I opened her belly with the longer sword to reach the man who had been standing behind her. He tried to swing his chopping sword and I shot him through the chest before the swing came all the way around. I moved deeper into the room.

Cut and thrust. The sword went in and came out wet. The blade work was faster than the revolver from close quarters, and the crystal floor was getting slick enough that footing was part of the arithmetic now. One round left — I had a read on it without looking. I held it.

I was swinging at a swordsman's ankles when a cudgel caught the side of my helmet and the world rang. My knees went and I came down on the crystal floor hard, and I was on my back before I sorted out which direction the ceiling was. The man above me had a bar of crystal raised; I could see the underside of it. Then a bayonet came through his chest from behind and he folded forward and went down beside me instead.

The blond merc was standing where the man had been. He reached a hand down.

A hole opened in his forehead, in a spray, and his fingers went slack.

He dropped.

I got moving. I blocked a sabre thrust and cut the swordsman across the ankle and he went to one knee and I shot him through the groin with the last round and he went down the rest of the way. Empty now. I pushed to my feet and someone had his arms around my chest from behind before I was all the way up, and I could not get either weapon into the angle. I got my gauntlet hand into his face instead and poured power into the contact through the interface. The flesh sizzled and cooked under my palm, and the burn came away wet and oozing when he tore himself back. The Powerstone read dropped further than I liked. He let go to clutch at the wound and I ran the longer sword through his chest when I stepped back from him.

Quiet.

Not completely. The redhead with the spear was on her feet, tracking. Her partner had one pistol reloaded and was standing with it levelled. One of the surviving gantry mercs had his bayonet on the last man in the room, who had dropped his weapon and gone to his knees with his hands over his head.

"No please —"

The redhead stepped up and put the pistol barrel an inch from his forehead. The shot was very loud in a room that had gone mostly quiet. He slid sideways and lay still on the crystal floor.

Then there was only the breathing, and the dripping, and the remaining mercs moving methodically along the wounded on the floor. Blades into throats, bayonets under ribs. The ones who belonged to the other side of the contract stopped making noise.

The target was not here. I checked the room: the men we had killed were armed labour and a handful of melee fighters, no one with the look of the man whose name Ford had given me. Fled before we breached, or dead before the breach party reached this building, or among the bodies in a way that was not going to be clear until someone turned them over in daylight. Not my work anymore tonight.

I crossed to the window.

The courtyard below was lit by two glow-globe lanterns on poles, throwing small circles of light on the crystal cobblestones. Detective Ford stood near the dry fountain at the centre, the kind of fountain where the mechanism had run out of mana years back and nobody had contracted to fix it. He had his coat buttoned. He was talking to someone I could not see, his back to the lanterns, his face tilted slightly down. The cluster of captives, women from the cooking floors, from the hall, from wherever the cartel had kept them, were off to Ford's right, guarded by two of his grey-coat men.

I sheathed the sword. I picked the primary revolver off the floor where I had dropped it, confirmed the empty cylinder, and holstered it. I reached up and undid the clasp at the back of the helmet and lifted it off.

The night air came through the window frame and the sweat on my forehead and the back of my neck went cold in it.

I touched the gem on the gauntlet's interface panel, cycling through the preset options until I found what I wanted. The gorget contracted, pulling mana back into the Powerstone as it shrank to the choker at my throat. The corset followed: a steady dissolving that started at the bottom hem and worked up, cool air replacing crystal plating as it went, the relief of weight off my shoulders and ribcage something I noticed each time and never stopped noticing. I was down to bra, thong, and coat. I unbuttoned the coat and lifted the tail and let the night air go where the corset had been.

The Powerstone reading was still low, even with what the corset had given back. The gauntlet burn had cost.

Down in the courtyard, Ford was still talking. One of the grey-coat men had drifted away from the captive cluster. He crossed to the far edge of the lantern light, where the shadow of the building made a narrow angle against the crystal wall. One of the women from the cluster was over there, not moving. The grey-coat man grabbed her by the hair and moved her further into the shadow and she started struggling. He backhanded her once across the side of the head and put her legs apart with his knee.

Ford did not turn around.

The man was done before two minutes had passed. Another of the grey-coat men crossed the courtyard toward the shadow. Ford had filed it under the cost column and moved on with the rest of his evening.

I leaned against the window frame and let the night air do its work.

More men went toward the shadow at the base of the wall. I watched the courtyard long enough to know what it was and then I let my eyes go to the glow-globe lanterns and the dry fountain and the far wall instead.

The Powerstone at my throat held what it held. I stayed where I was, in the cold coming off the courtyard, and let the night go on without me for a while.
