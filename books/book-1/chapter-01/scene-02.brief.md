### Fix — the seven concrete revisions

**1. The shield as an on-page obstacle that costs.**
Scene-01 ends with Kyra under the arch, thumb on the projector-warded seal, shield-breaker primed. Scene-02 must open with that beat playing through — not with the shield already dropped. Show the inner-sanctum shield as it actually is: a shimmer across the arch, a visible barrier, audible hum, dense enough to stop a body. Show Kyra fire the shield-breaker. Show the cost: the projector cracks along with the shield, the device is permanently spent, Kendall's warning ("save it for projectors worth the cost") earns its weight. The shield-breaker does not vanish off-page. The discharge is a real act with a real consequence.

**2. The struggle through the drug den. Multiple rooms, brutal, fast.**
The source manuscript has Kyra fighting through **the drug den** as a sustained sequence — not one room. The rewrite collapsed it into a single bayonet-team encounter and lost the texture. Restore the multi-room trek:
- After the shield-breaker discharge, the immediate next space is a short connecting corridor or antechamber with one or two defenders. Fast, brutal, voice-correct.
- Then a larger room — possibly the cooking-rig hall — with more guards (mix of muzzle-loaders and the cheaper hire) and a melee component. Kyra uses the sword here. Crystal bullets are expensive; blade work is the default for finishing wounded or close-range work.
- Then the bayonet team room (the one with the illegal ley-line tap) — see fix #4 below.
- Then a connecting hall to the upper-level stairs.
The fight should be **fraught with magic, gunplay, and sword work in alternation**, not just one mode. Spells from her gauntlet (small directional things — a shove, a heat-blast, a deflection), revolver rounds reserved for cleaner shots, sword for finishes. Brutal, visceral, fast-paced.

**3. Kyra's armour saves her life several times — and her gear sets her apart.**
The source establishes Kyra as **much better-geared than both her allies and her opponents**. This is part of why she is alive at the end and they are not, and the reader has to feel it. Show:
- A round catches her gorget and deflects in a spray of indigo dust; an unarmoured merc would be dead.
- A bayonet glances off the corset (the materialised-magic armour over her ribs) instead of opening her belly.
- A spell or close-range round that would have killed Anton or an ordinary cartel guard meets her armour and bleeds harmlessly off.
- Voice register on each: clinical, professional, not relieved. ("That would have killed someone in less gear. I noted it and moved on.") She is a merc who knows what she is wearing and what it does. Her gauntlet, her gorget, her corset, her helmet — Kendall's work, hand-fitted, premium-grade. Off-duty Guard moonlighters have Guard-issue plate; the cookers and cheap hires have nothing. The contrast is voice-correct without being a tutorial. **Land at least three armour-saves across the scene.**

**4. The dispenser-room direct-cast spell.**
In the room with the jury-rigged mana dispenser / illegal ley-line tap, the fight should reach a **deadlock** — Kyra pinned behind cover, enemy positioning that her revolver can't solve. The illegal tap is **bleeding ambient mana into this one room** that doesn't exist elsewhere on the Skylands. Kyra draws on **that ambient field directly** — not her Powerstone, not a cast through the gauntlet, but raw ambient drawn into a held spell from cover. Use it to break the deadlock: a directional shove, a fire-blast, a stun, an arc of force that takes out the cover the enemy is using. Voice register: she registers the difference — this is the first time she's ever cast from ambient because the Skylands does not normally allow it. It is a brief, professional noticing, not a thesis. (Later in the book she will encounter mainland natural ambient and the moment will land harder; this is the seed.)

**5. Anton's death. Sudden, gory, emotionless.**
Anton catches up to Kyra at some point in the trek (between rooms — he arrives still in graft-flood, drenched, the borrowed-blood seam-scars still running). He helps clear one room, possibly. Then he **dies, suddenly**. A cartel guard with a muzzle-loader takes a shot from somewhere Kyra didn't see — the round catches Anton in the back of the head and the front of his skull comes apart on its way out. He drops mid-stride. **The voice does not editorialise.** "Anton went down. I noted him. I kept moving." Or words to that exact register. No grief. No relief. No tactical assessment of what his death costs her. The graft-flood ends with him. The body is on the floor. Kyra continues.

**6. No mercy. No prisoners. No surrender-offers. Blade for finishes.**
The "Drop it. You can walk." dialog from draft-1 was wrong. Pre-Anjali Kyra does not negotiate; she does not offer terms; she does not take prisoners. The mercenary register on this raid is **kill everything that is still a threat or could become one**. Specifically:
- **No surrender-offers.** No "drop it" / "you can walk" / "stand down." If a guard surrenders or drops a weapon, she may register that and still kill him, because witnesses are a liability.
- **Crystal bullets are expensive.** She doesn't waste them. Wounded enemies are finished with the sword (cheaper, quieter, also voice-correct for the merc economy). She'll **double-tap** with a round if the wounded enemy is still actively dangerous — but the default for a downed enemy is the blade.
- **Casual executions.** Step on a wrist. Sword through the throat. Kneel and cut. The professional accounting, no lyric, no apology.
- This is pre-Anjali Kyra — the brutal-jaded register the canon locked in. The voice does not soften any of this.

**7. The shield-breaker cost paid.**
Tied to fix #1 — the shield-breaker is the load-bearing introduction of "things cost money in Skylander combat." The projector cracks; the device is dead; Kendall will want to know what she did with it (she'll lie); the next inner-room shield she encounters (whatever the cartel uses in the grand hall or the upper rooms) she will have to crack the hard way. The reader should feel the cost.
